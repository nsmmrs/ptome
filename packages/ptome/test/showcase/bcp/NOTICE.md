# The Book of Common Prayer

The Calendar, the Order for Morning Prayer, the Litany and the Psalter
as John Baskerville printed them at Cambridge in 1761 (the octavo of
1760 reissued), page for page and line for line (`tool/bcp_1761`).
What the work shows: a prayer book's pages, each a section of its own
(`:source-lines:`, each source line a printed line) in a frame of
printers' flowers drawn behind it (`section_role_<role>_page_background_image`,
`images/frame.svg`, traced from the scan), the running head the
day's number flush right on a recto and flush left on a verso; the
psalms' titles, `PSAL.` and its number with the Latin in italic; the
offices' heads in the Psalter, Morning and Evening Prayer in spaced
italic capitals; psalm cxix.'s twenty-two parts under their Latin; each
verse's number in its first line, the first verse of each psalm opening
with an initial two lines deep, the rest of its word in spaced
capitals; the rubrics in a smaller italic, their ¶ hung; the Litany's
responses after its petitions, the versicles and their answers set in
where the printing sets them; the titles of Morning Prayer and the
Psalter in their sizes over a double rule; the signature and the
catchword at the foot, on the page's last line where it is short; and
the Calendar's twelve months, each a ruled table on a page of its own,
its rules drawn where the printing's are, a day to a row at the month's
own pitch, a cell's rule a leader of rules
(`role_<role>_leader`), a name the printing set closer drawn together.
Each block's space above it and its pitch are the scan's.

The pages (`text/pages.adoc`) are generated by `tool/bcp_1761/gen.py`
from `bcp.spec` and `calendar.spec`: each line of the scan read by
Tesseract, its words decided against the corpus's (the corpus's
letters where they are the scan's, the printing's own spelling where it
differs, the long s read as the round), the words no rule decides read
off the scan, then each line read against the scan where it is not
the corpus's (`tool/bcp_1761/fixes.tsv`); Morning Prayer, the Litany and
the Psalter's title page written whole by hand from the scan
(`tool/bcp_1761/pages/`); the Calendar's months read off the scan whole.
Each foot's signature is the one the octavo's collation gives.

## Fonts

Baskervville, the Atelier national de recherche typographique's revival
of Baskerville's types, and EB Garamond for the versicle sign and the
pointing hand (SIL Open Font License 1.1), from Google Fonts: `ptome
doctor --look bcp --looksdir test/showcase/bcp` installs them. Not
committed.

## Where it departs from the printing

- The long s is set as the round s.
- Baskervville stands in for Baskerville's own types: its letters are
  not his, its widths a little different, so a justified line's spaces
  differ from the printing's (its letters may widen or narrow by up to
  four percent, `base_font_expansion`).
- The frame of flowers is one leaf's (417), traced, on every page; the
  printing's frame is set from the type of flowers and differs a little
  from page to page.
- The octavo is printed in black alone, as the work is.

## In HTML and EPUB

- HTML and EPUB have the text line for line, without the frames, the
  Calendar's rules or the pages' places.
