[.salutation,margin-top=2.76pt]
_Courteous Reader,_

[.dropcap,margin-top=-1.34pt]
I Might in this place attempt to gain thy Favour,
by declaring that I write Almanacks with no
other View than that of the publick Good; but in
this I should not be sincere; and Men are now a-{zwsp}
days too wise to be deceiv’d by Pretences how spe{shy}
cious soever. The plain Truth of the Matter is, I
am excessive poor, and my Wife, good Woman,
is, I tell her, excessive proud; she cannot bear, she
says, to sit spinning in her Shift of Tow, while I
do nothing but gaze at the Stars; and has threatned
more than once to burn all my Books and Rattling-{zwsp}
Traps (as she calls my Instruments) if I do not
make some profitable Use of them for the good of
my Family. The Printer has offer’d me some con{shy}
siderable share of the Profits, and I have thus be{shy}
gun to comply with my Dame’s desire.

[.prose,margin-top=0.22pt]
Indeed this Motive would have had Force enough
to have made me publish an Almanack many Years
since, had it not been overpower’d by my Regard
for my good Friend and Fellow-Student, Mr. _Titan_
_Leeds,_ whose Interest I was extreamly unwilling to
hurt: But this Obstacle (I am far from speaking it
with Pleasure) is soon to be removed, since inexo{shy}

[#explanation.front-plain%notitle,folio-start=5]
== Explanation of this ALMANACK.

[.ex-head,margin-top=2.51pt]
Explanation of this ALMANACK.

[.dropcap,margin-top=4.74pt]
THE first Column shews the Days of the Month.
The second shews the Week day, _Sunday_
Letter being this Year G.

[.prose,margin-top=-0.88pt]
The third contains the Days observ’d by the Church,
the Aspects of the Planets and Judgment of the
Weather; the Length, Increase, and Decrease of
Days, the rising and setting of the seven Stars, &c.

[.prose,margin-top=-0.51pt]
The fourth is the Time of High Water at _Phi{shy}_
_ladelphia,_ (h) signifies _half an hour after._

[.prose]
The fifth is the Moon’s Place.

[.prose,margin-top=-0.45pt]
The sixth is the Sun’s rising and setting, thus un{shy}
derstood; Against the second day of _January_ you
see in the 6th Column these Figures 7 14 5, which
show that the Sun rises that Day 14 minutes after 7,
and sets 14 minutes before 5. I have chose to put
the Sun’s rising and setting for every day, rather than
the Moon’s, because of its constant Use in setting
of Clocks and Watches.

[.prose,margin-top=-0.99pt]
In the last Column, with the Changes of the
Moon, I have put her rising and setting every five
or six Days: If you want to know her rising or set{shy}
ting on a Day or two after That against which I
have set it, the common Rule of an hour and half
later each day, will be exact enough to direct Peo{shy}
ple in their Travelling, which is the common Use
that is made of it.
