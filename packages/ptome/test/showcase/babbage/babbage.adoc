== Advertisement

The logarithms that follow were computed for this book, to seven
places, and are set after Charles Babbage's _Table of Logarithms of the
Natural Numbers, from 1 to 108000_ (London, 1827), which was printed to
be read with the least fatigue to the eye and the fewest errors in
copying.

Each row holds the logarithms of ten numbers: the first four figures of
the numbers stand in the column marked N., the fifth heads the column
in which the logarithm stands. The first column gives the whole
logarithm, its first three figures apart; the other columns give its
last four figures, the first three being taken from the first column of
the row; where those three change within a row, the figures after the
change are marked, and the first three are those of the row below. The
column marked D. gives the difference between the logarithms of two
consecutive numbers in the row, in units of the seventh place, for
taking proportional parts.

The rows are set in fives, with a space between each five, so that the
eye does not wander from one row to the next; the figures are of one
width, so that they stand in their columns; the heads of the columns are
repeated at the top of every page, and the numbers a page holds are
given in its head. The paper is tinted, after Babbage's trials of
printing the table on paper of many colours.

Only the characteristic, which is 4 for every number here, is left to
the reader.
