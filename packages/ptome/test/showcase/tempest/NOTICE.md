# The Tempest

Shakespeare's The Tempest in two looks over one source: a modern reading
edition's (`tempest`) and the First Folio's (`folio`). What the work
shows: a play as description lists (each speech its speaker's), stage
directions as a role; the same text set in two ways, by the look alone;
in the modern look, a verse line shared between two speakers stepped so
that its parts read as one line (`role_<role>_text_indent: continue`,
FEAT-d1sdfw), each scene's lines numbered every fifth, the heads, names
and directions not counted and a shared line counted once
(`line_numbers_restart_level`, `line_numbers_skip_*`). The Folio's look
sets the Folio's own text, as the First Folio sets it, its nineteen
pages page for page and line for line (`tool/tempest_1623`): each source
line a printed line (`:source-lines:`), each page a section in its frame
of rules (`section_role_<role>_page_background_image`), the first and
the last set across the page over their columns
(`section_role_<role>_page_columns: 1`), the last page's bands
(`[.columns]` blocks), the words too long for a line turned up or down
after a bracket (`role_<role>_turnover`), the lines the compositor
squeezed set closer, each column on the scan's pitch.

Imported by `tool/showcase_import.dart` (each speech table a description
list, a row without a speaker a `[.direction]` paragraph, or a
`[.continued]` one where the speaker goes on after a direction), then
edited:

- `tempest.adoc`: the line numbers for the modern look only (`ifeval` on
  the look).
- 157 verse lines shared between two speakers marked `[.shared]##…##`.
  Standard Ebooks doesn't mark them: they were found by the test editors
  use, two short half-lines next to each other that make one line of
  verse (each half of eight syllables or fewer, the shorter of five or
  fewer, nine to eleven together), and may include a few that an
  editor would read otherwise.
- `images/folio-frame.svg`: the Folio's frame of rules, drawn for the
  work, its size the Boston copy's page measured from the scan;
  `images/tempest-brace.svg`, the brace in the list of the actors.

The Folio's text (`text/folio.adoc`) is generated by
`tool/tempest_1623/gen.py` from `folio.spec`: the transcription's words
laid on the scan's lines (Tesseract's) and read against the scan line by
line, corrected where the transcription runs two printed lines together
or the scan reads otherwise (`fixes.txt`, 48 corrections: the feet's
signatures and catchwords, which the transcription leaves out, the
letters it marks illegible, the turned words, the last page). Its
spelling, long s, ligatures (*Scœna*), abbreviations (*yͤ*, *ſtāding*),
italics and speech prefixes are the Folio's.

## Licence

The play is in the public domain (first printed in 1623). Standard
Ebooks dedicates its production to the public domain (CC0 1.0); its own

The Text Creation Partnership's transcriptions of EEBO's texts are

## The printing the Folio look follows

Mr. William Shakespeares Comedies, Histories, & Tragedies, London:
Isaac Jaggard and Ed. Blount, 1623 (the First Folio), the Boston Public
Library's copy: https://archive.org/details/mrvvilliamshakes00shak

The modern look follows no one printing: it is designed after the
reading editions (the Folger's speakers on a line of their own and its
stepped shared lines; the line numbers every fifth by scene, as the
Oxford and the Arden count them).

## Where they depart

- The two looks set two texts: the modern look Standard Ebooks'
  modernized Globe text, its cast first; the Folio's look the Folio's
  own, its cast ("Names of the Actors") at the play's end.
- The Folio's look: IM Fell DW Pica stands in for the Folio's types, at
  the sizes that give the Folio's line widths; its words' widths differ
  from the Folio's by a few percent, so an unjustified line ends a
  little short of or past the Folio's (each line's baseline is within
  1.7 points of the scan's, its start within 3 for nearly all:
  `tool/tempest_1623/README.md`). Its ornaments are the Boston copy's,
  cut from the scan.
- HTML and EPUB: a shared line is set in by a fixed amount (they can't
  measure the line before it), and the lines aren't numbered; the
  Folio's look is a page's: its HTML and EPUB set the same lines,
  without its pages, frames and places.
