# New-York Tribune

The first two pages of the New-York Tribune of Tuesday, 16 April 1912,
the morning after the Titanic sank, as the paper prints them, page for
page and line for line (`tool/tribune_1912`). What the work shows: a
broadsheet's pages, each a section of its own (`:source-lines:`, each
source line a printed line), the page the scan's size: the title cut
from the scan over the folio line, its parts where the paper's are; the
banner across seven columns; the columns ruled apart, the stories in the
cells of tables whose columns are the scan's, spanning two or four
columns where the paper's do; each head in its face (an extra condensed
gothic, a condensed gothic, a bold or a light roman) and each of its
lines its own size, the size the scan's line's width gives it; the text
in each column's own size and measure, each paragraph's space above it
and its pitch the scan's; the tally of the lost with dots led to its
figures (`role_<role>_leader`); the lists of survivors in a gothic,
spaced as the paper spaces them; the map and the pictures, the ship's
longitudinal section and two advertisements cut from the scan, each
where the paper sets it. 846 of its 864 lines lie within a point of the
scan's.

The pages (`text/pages.adoc`) are generated by `tool/tribune_1912/gen.py`
from each region's spec (`tool/tribune_1912/regions/PAGE-REGION.spec`):
the front page's stories as the work transcribed them before
(`tool/tribune_1912/transcription/`) laid on the scan's lines, and the
second page's columns, the heads and the advertisements read off the
scan. The transcription keeps the paper's words, spelling ("to-day",
"pasenger", "Titantic"), capitals and punctuation, and the wireless's
garbled names in the lists of survivors; it corrects only the OCR.

## The printing the work follows

The New-York Tribune, Tuesday, 16 April 1912, its first and second
pages, scanned for Chronicling America by the Library of Congress
(reel 00206531526), https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030214/1912-04-16/ed-1/.

Where it departs from it:

- Old Standard TT, Fira Sans Condensed, League Gothic and Libre Franklin
  stand in for the paper's romans and gothics; the banner is set in Old
  Standard TT's bold, wider than the paper's condensed bold, so its
  letters are shorter.
- The microfilmed pages lean and bow a little, each column its own way;
  the work's lines are level, so a line's ends lie up to two points
  above or below the scan's where its middle lies on it.
- The rules round the folio line and the advertisements' frames are
  left out; the rules between the columns are the tables'.
- In HTML and EPUB the pages are the tables' cells in order, without
  the pages' sizes or places.
