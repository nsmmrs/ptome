= Westminster Larger Catechism
:units: catechism
:works: bible=../bible/kjv/kjv.adoc

Westminster Assembly, 1647. Public Domain; text from Creeds.json.

@ What is the chief and highest end of man?

Man's chief and highest end is to glorify God,note:proof[<<Rom 11:36; 1 Cor 10:31>>] and fully to enjoy him forever.note:proof[<<Ps 73:24–28>>]

@ How doth it appear that there is a God?

The very light of nature in man, and the works of God, declare plainly that there is a God;note:proof[<<Rom 1:19–20; Ps 19:1–3; Acts 17:28>>] but his word and Spirit only do sufficiently and effectually reveal him unto men for their salvation.note:proof[<<1 Cor 2:9–1 Cor 2:10; 2 Tim 3:15–2 Tim 3:17; Isa 59:21>>]

@ What is the word of God?

The holy scriptures of the Old and New Testaments are the word of God,note:proof[<<2 Tim 3:16; 2 Pet 1:19–2 Pet 1:21>>] the only rule of faith and obedience.note:proof[<<Eph 2:20; Rev 22:18–19; Isa 8:20; Luke 16:29; Luke 16:31; Gal 1:8–9; 2 Tim 3:15–2 Tim 3:16>>]

@ How doth it appear that the scriptures are the word of God?

The scriptures manifest themselves to be the word of God, by their majesty note:proof[<<Hos 8:12; 1 Cor 2:6–1 Cor 2:7; 1 Cor 2:13; Ps 119:18; Ps 119:129>>] and purity;note:proof[<<Ps 12:6; Ps 119:140>>] by the consent of all the parts,note:proof[<<Acts 10:43; Acts 26:22>>] and the scope of the whole, which is to give all glory to God;note:proof[<<Rom 3:19; Rom 3:27>>] by their light and power to convince and convert sinners, to comfort and build up believers unto salvation:note:proof[<<Acts 23:28; Heb 4:12; Jas 1:18; Ps 19:7–9; Rom 15:4; Acts 20:32>>] but the Spirit of God bearing witness by and with the scriptures in the heart of man, is alone able fully to persuade it that they are the very word of God.note:proof[<<John 16:13–14; John 20:31; 1 John 2:20; 1 John 2:27>>]

@ What do the scriptures principally teach?

The scriptures principally teach, what man is to believe concerning God, and what duty God requires of man.note:proof[<<2 Tim 1:13>>]

@ What do the scriptures make known of God?

The scriptures make known what God is,note:proof[<<Heb 11:6>>] the persons in the Godhead,note:proof[<<1 John 5:7>>] his decrees,note:proof[<<Acts 15:14–15; Acts 15:18>>] and the execution of his decrees.note:proof[<<Acts 4:27–28>>]

@ What is God?

God is a Spirit,note:proof[<<John 4:24>>] in and of himself infinite in being,note:proof[<<Exod 3:14; Job 11:7–9>>] glory,note:proof[<<Acts 7:2>>] blessedness,note:proof[<<1 Tim 6:1>>] and perfection;note:proof[<<Matt 5:48>>] all-sufficient,note:proof[<<Gen 17:1>>] eternal,note:proof[<<Ps 90:2>>] unchangeable,note:proof[<<Mal 3:6; Jas 1:17>>] incomprehensible,note:proof[<<1 Kgs 8:27>>] everywhere present,note:proof[<<Ps 139:1–13>>] almighty,note:proof[<<Rev 4:8>>] knowing all things,note:proof[<<Heb 4:13; Ps 147:5>>] most wise,note:proof[<<Rom 16:27>>] most holy,note:proof[<<Isa 6:3; Rev 15:4>>] most just,note:proof[<<Deut 32:4>>] most merciful and gracious, long-suffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.note:proof[<<Exod 34:6>>]

@ Are there more Gods than one?

There is but one only, the living and true God.note:proof[<<Deut 6:4; 1 Cor 8:4; 1 Cor 8:6; Jer 10:10>>]

@ How many persons are there in the Godhead?

There be three persons in the Godhead, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost; and these three are one true, eternal God, the same in substance, equal in power and glory; although distinguished by their personal properties.note:proof[<<1 John 5:7; Matt 3:16–17; Matt 28:19; 2 Cor 13:14; John 10:30>>]

@ What are the personal properties of the three persons in the Godhead?

It is proper to the Father to beget the Son,note:proof[<<Heb 1:5–6; Heb 1:8>>] and to the Son to be begotten of the Father,note:proof[<<John 1:14; John 1:18>>] and to the Holy Ghost to proceed from the Father and the Son from all eternity.note:proof[<<John 15:26; Gal 4:6>>]

@ How doth it appear that the Son and the Holy Ghost are God equal with the Father?

The scriptures manifest that the Son and the Holy Ghost are God equal with the Father, ascribing unto them such names,note:proof[<<Isa 6:3; Isa 6:5; Isa 6:8; John 12:41; Acts 5:3–4; Acts 28:25; 1 John 5:20>>] attributes,note:proof[<<John 1:1; John 2:24–25; Isa 9:6; 1 Cor 2:10–1 Cor 2:11>>] works,note:proof[<<Col 1:16; Gen 1:2>>] and worship,note:proof[<<Matt 28:19; 2 Cor 13:14>>] as are proper to God only.

@ What are the decrees of God?

God's decrees are the wise, free, and holy acts of the counsel of his will,note:proof[<<Eph 1:11; Rom 9:14–15; Rom 9:18; Rom 11:33>>] whereby, from all eternity, he hath, for his own glory, unchangeably foreordained: Whatsoever comes to pass in time,note:proof[<<Eph 1:4; Eph 1:11; Rom 9:22–23; Ps 33:11>>] especially concerning angels and men.

@ What hath God especially decreed concerning angels and men?

God, by an eternal and immutable decree, out of his mere love, for the praise of his glorious grace, to be manifested in due time, hath elected some angels to glory;note:proof[<<1 Tim 5:21>>] and in Christ hath chosen some men to eternal life, and the means thereof:note:proof[<<Eph 1:4–6; 2 Thess 2:13–2 Thess 2:14>>] and also, according to his sovereign power, and the unsearchable counsel of his own will (whereby he extendeth or withholdeth favor as he pleases), hath passed by and foreordained the rest to dishonor and wrath, to be for their sin inflicted, to the praise of the glory of his justice.note:proof[<<Rom 9:17–18; Rom 9:21–22; Matt 11:25–26; 2 Tim 2:20; Jude 1:4; 1 Pet 2:8>>]

@ How doth God execute his decrees?

God executeth his decrees in the works of creation and providence, according to his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable counsel of his own will.note:proof[<<Eph 1:11>>]

@ What is the work of creation?

The work of creation is that wherein God did in the beginning, by the word of his power, make of nothing the world, and all things therein, for himself, within the space of six days, and all very good.note:proof[<<Gen 1; Heb 11:8; Prov 16:4>>]

@ How did God create angels?

God created all the angelsnote:proof[<<Col 1:16>>] spirits,note:proof[<<Ps 104:4>>] immortal,note:proof[<<Matt 22:30>>] holy,note:proof[<<Matt 25:31>>] excelling in knowledge,note:proof[<<2 Sam 14:17; Matt 24:36>>] mighty in power,note:proof[<<2 Thess 1:7>>] to execute his commandments, and to praise his name,note:proof[<<Ps 103:20–21>>] yet subject to change.note:proof[<<2 Pet 2:4>>]

@ How did God create man?

After God had made all other creatures, he created man male and female;note:proof[<<Gen 1:27>>] formed the body of the man of the dust of the ground,note:proof[<<Gen 2:7>>] and the woman of the rib of the man,note:proof[<<Gen 2:22>>] endued them with living, reasonable, and immortal souls;note:proof[<<Gen 2:7; Job 35:11; Eccl 12:7; Matt 10:28; Luke 23:43>>] made them after his own image,note:proof[<<Gen 1:27>>] in knowledge,note:proof[<<Col 3:10>>] righteousness,and holiness;note:proof[<<Eph 4:24>>] having the law of God written in their hearts,note:proof[<<Rom 2:14–15>>] and power to fulfil it,note:proof[<<Eccl 7:29>>] and dominion over the creatures;note:proof[<<Gen 1:28>>] yet subject to fall.note:proof[<<Gen 3:6; Eccl 7:29>>]

@ What are God's works of providence?

God's works of providence are his most holy,note:proof[<<Ps 145:17>>] wise,note:proof[<<Ps 104:24; Isa 28:29>>] and powerful preserving note:proof[<<Heb 1:8>>] and governing note:proof[<<Ps 103:19>>] all his creatures; ordering them, and all their actions,note:proof[<<Matt 10:29–31; Gen 45:7>>] to his own glory.note:proof[<<Rom 11:36; Isa 43:14>>]

@ What is God's providence towards the angels?

God by his providence permitted some of the angels, wilfully and irrecoverably, to fall into sin and damnation,note:proof[<<Jude 1:6; 2 Pet 2:4; Heb 2:16; John 8:44>>] limiting and ordering that, and all their sins, to his own glory;note:proof[<<Job 1:12; Matt 8:31>>] and established the rest in holiness and happiness;note:proof[<<1 Tim 5:21; Mark 8:38; Heb 12:22>>] employing them all,note:proof[<<Ps 104:4>>] at his pleasure, in the administrations of his power, mercy, and justice.note:proof[<<2 Kgs 19:35; Heb 1:14>>]

@ What was the providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was created?

The providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was created, was the placing him in paradise, appointing him to dress it, giving him liberty to eat of the fruit of the earth;note:proof[<<Gen 2:8; Gen 2:15–16>>] putting the creatures under his dominion,note:proof[<<Gen 1:28>>] and ordaining marriage for his help;note:proof[<<Gen 2:18>>] affording him communion with himself;note:proof[<<Gen 1:26–29; Gen 3:8>>] instituting the sabbath;note:proof[<<Gen 2:3>>] entering into a covenant of life with him, upon condition of personal, perfect, and perpetual obedience,note:proof[<<Gal 3:12; Rom 10:5>>] of which the tree of life was a pledge;note:proof[<<Gen 2:9>>] and forbidding to eat of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, upon the pain of death.note:proof[<<Gen 2:17>>]

@ Did man continue in that estate wherein God at first created him?

Our first parents being left to the freedom of their own will, through the temptation of Satan, transgressed the commandment of God in eating the forbidden fruit; and thereby fell from the estate of innocency wherein they were created.note:proof[<<Gen 3:6–8; Gen 3:13; Eccl 7:29; 2 Cor 11:3>>]

@ Did all mankind fall in that first transgression ?

The covenant being made with Adam as a public person, not for himself only, but for his posterity, all mankind descending from him by ordinary generation,note:proof[<<Acts 17:26>>] sinned in him, and fell with him in that first transgression.note:proof[<<Gen 2:16–17; Rom 5:12–20; 1 Cor 15:21–1 Cor 15:22>>]

@ Into what estate did the fall bring mankind?

The fall brought mankind into an estate of sin and misery.note:proof[<<Rom 3:23; Rom 5:12>>]

@ What is sin?

Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, any law of God, given as a rule to the reasonable creature.note:proof[<<1 John 3:4; Gal 3:10; Gal 3:12>>]

@ Wherein consists the sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell?

The sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell, consisteth in the guilt of Adam's first sin,note:proof[<<Rom 5:12; Rom 5:19>>] the want of that righteousness wherein he was created, and the corruption of his nature, whereby he is utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite unto all that is spiritually good, and wholly inclined to all evil, and that continually;note:proof[<<Rom 3:10–19; Rom 5:6; Rom 8:7–8; Eph 2:1–3; Gen 6:5>>] which is commonly called Original Sin, and from which do proceed all actual transgressions.note:proof[<<Jas 1:14–15; Matt 15:19>>]

@ How is original sin conveyed from our first parents unto their posterity?

Original sin is conveyed from our first parents unto their posterity by natural generation, so as all that proceed from them in that way are conceived and born in sin.note:proof[<<Ps 51:5; Job 14:4; Job 15:14; John 3:6>>]

@ What misery did the fall bring upon mankind?

The fall brought upon mankind the loss of communion with God,note:proof[<<Gen 3:8; Gen 3:10; Gen 3:24>>] his displeasure and curse; so as we are by nature children of wrath,note:proof[<<Eph 2:2–3>>] bond slaves to Satan,note:proof[<<2 Tim 2:26>>] and justly liable to all punishments in this world, and that which is to come.note:proof[<<Gen 2:17; Lam 3:39; Rom 6:23; Matt 25:41; Matt 25:46; Jude 1:7>>]

@ What are the punishments of sin in this world?

The punishments of sin in this world are either inward, as blindness of mind,note:proof[<<Eph 4:18>>] a reprobate sense,note:proof[<<Rom 1:28>>] strong delusions,note:proof[<<2 Thess 2:11>>] hardness of heart,note:proof[<<Rom 2:5>>] horror of conscience,note:proof[<<Isa 33:14; Gen 4:13; Matt 27:4>>] and vile affections;note:proof[<<Rom 1:26>>] or outward, as the curse of God upon the creatures for our sakes,note:proof[<<Gen 3:17>>] and all other evils that befall us in our bodies, names, estates, relations, and employments;note:proof[<<Deut 28:15–18>>] together with death itself.note:proof[<<Rom 6:21; Rom 6:23>>]

@ What are the punishments of sin in the world to come?

The punishments of sin in the world to come, are everlasting separation from the comfortable presence of God, and most grievous torments in soul and body, without intermission, in hell fire forever.note:proof[<<2 Thess 1:9; Mark 9:43–44; Mark 9:46; Mark 9:48; Luke 16:24>>]

@ Doth God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?

God doth not leave all men to perish in the estate of sin and misery,note:proof[<<1 Thess 5:9>>] into which they fell by the breach of the first covenant, commonly called the Covenant of Works;note:proof[<<Gal 3:10; Gal 3:12>>] but of his mere love and mercy delivereth his elect out of it, and bringeth them into an estate of salvation by the second covenant, commonly called the Covenant of Grace.note:proof[<<Titus 3:4–7; Gal 3:21; Rom 3:20–22>>]

@ With whom was the covenant of grace made?

The covenant of grace was made with Christ as the second Adam, and in him with all the elect as his seed.note:proof[<<Gal 3:16; Rom 5:15–21; Isa 53:10–11>>]

@ How is the grace of God manifested in the second covenant?

The grace of God is manifested in the second covenant, in that he freely provideth and offereth to sinners a Mediator,note:proof[<<Gen 3:15; Isa 42:6; John 6:27>>] and life and salvation by him;note:proof[<<1 John 5:11–1 John 5:12>>] and requiring faith as the condition to interest them in him, promiseth and giveth his Holy Spirit note:proof[<<John 1:12; John 3:16>>] to all his elect, to work in them that faith,note:proof[<<Prov 1:23>>] with all other saving graces;note:proof[<<2 Cor 4:13>>] and to enable them unto all holy obedience,note:proof[<<2 Cor 4:13>>] as the evidence of the truth of their faith note:proof[<<Gal 5:22–23>>] and thankfulness to God,note:proof[<<Ezek 36:27>>] and as the way which he hath appointed them to salvation.note:proof[<<Jas 2:18; Jas 2:22>>]

@ Was the covenant of grace always administered after one and the same manner?

The covenant of grace was not always administered after the same manner, but the administrations of it under the Old Testament were different from those under the New.note:proof[<<2 Cor 3:6–2 Cor 3:9>>]

@ How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament?

The covenant of grace was administered under the Old Testament, by promises,note:proof[<<Rom 15:8>>] prophecies, note:proof[<<Acts 3:20; Acts 3:24>>] sacrifices,note:proof[<<Heb 10:1>>] circumcision,note:proof[<<Rom 4:11>>] the passover,note:proof[<<1 Cor 5:7>>] and other types and ordinances, which did all foresignify Christ then to come, and were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah,note:proof[<<Heb 8–Heb 10; Heb 11:13>>] by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation.note:proof[<<Gal 3:7–9; Gal 3:14>>]

@ How is the covenant of grace administered under the New Testament?

Under the New Testament, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the same covenant of grace was and still is to be administered in the preaching of the word,note:proof[<<Mark 16:15>>] and the administration of the sacraments of Baptism note:proof[<<Matt 28:19–20>>] and the Lord's Supper;note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:23–1 Cor 11:25>>] in which grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations.note:proof[<<2 Cor 3:6–2 Cor 3:9; Heb 8:6; Heb 8:10–11; Matt 28:19>>]

@ Who is the Mediator of the covenant of grace?

The only Mediator of the covenant of grace is the Lord Jesus Christ,note:proof[<<1 Tim 2:5>>] who, being the eternal Son of God, of one substance and equal with the Father,note:proof[<<John 1:1; John 1:14; John 10:30; Phil 2:6>>] in the fulness of time became man,note:proof[<<Gal 4:4>>] and so was and continues to be God and man, in two entire distinct natures, and one person, forever.note:proof[<<Luke 1:35; Rom 9:5; Col 2:9; Heb 7:24–25>>]

@ How did Christ, being the Son of God, become man?

Christ the Son of God became man, by taking to himself a true body, and a reasonable soul,note:proof[<<John 1:14; Matt 26:38>>] being conceived by the power of the Holy Ghost in the womb of the virgin Mary, of her substance, and born of her,note:proof[<<Luke 1:27; Luke 1:31; Luke 1:35; Luke 1:42; Gal 4:4>>] yet without sin.note:proof[<<Heb 4:15; Heb 7:26>>]

@ Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be God?

It was requisite that the Mediator should be God, that he might sustain and keep the human nature from sinking under the infinite wrath of God, and the power of death;note:proof[<<Acts 2:24–25; Rom 1:4; Rom 4:25; Heb 9:14>>] give worth and efficacy to his sufferings, obedience, and intercession;note:proof[<<Acts 20:28; Heb 7:25–28; Heb 9:14>>] and to satisfy God's justice,note:proof[<<Rom 3:24–26>>] procure his favor,note:proof[<<Eph 1:6; Matt 3:17>>] purchase a peculiar people,note:proof[<<Titus 2:13–14>>] give his Spirit to them,note:proof[<<Gal 4:6>>] conquer all their enemies,note:proof[<<Luke 1:68–69; Luke 1:71; Luke 1:74>>] and bring them to everlasting salvation.note:proof[<<Heb 5:8–9; Heb 9:11–15>>]

@ Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be man?

It was requisite that the Mediator should be man, that he might advance our nature,note:proof[<<Heb 2:16>>] perform obedience to the law,note:proof[<<Gal 4:4>>] suffer and make intercession for us in our nature,note:proof[<<Heb 2:14; Heb 7:24–25>>] have a fellow feeling of our infirmities;note:proof[<<Heb 4:15>>] that we might receive the adoption of sons,note:proof[<<Gal 4:5>>] and have comfort and access with boldness unto the throne of grace.note:proof[<<Heb 4:16>>]

@ Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be God and man in one person ?

It was requisite that the Mediator, who was to reconcile God and man, should himself be both God and man, and this in one person, that the proper works of each nature might be accepted of God for us, and relied on by us,note:proof[<<Matt 1:21; Matt 1:23; Matt 3:17; Heb 9:14>>] as the works of the whole person.note:proof[<<1 Pet 2:6>>]

@ Why was our Mediator called Jesus?

Our Mediator was called Jesus, because he saveth his people from their sins.note:proof[<<Matt 1:21>>]

@ Why was our Mediator called Christ?

Our Mediator was called Christ, because he was anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure;note:proof[<<John 3:34; Ps 45:7>>] and so set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability,note:proof[<<John 6:27; Matt 28:18–20>>] to execute the offices of prophet,note:proof[<<Acts 3:21–22; Luke 4:18; Luke 4:21>>] priest,note:proof[<<Heb 4:14–15; Heb 5:5–7>>] and king of his church,note:proof[<<Ps 2:6; Matt 21:5; Isa 9:6–7; Phil 2:8–11>>] in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation.

@ How doth Christ execute the office of a prophet?

Christ executeth the office of a prophet, in his revealing to the church,note:proof[<<John 1:18>>] in all ages, by his Spirit and word,note:proof[<<1 Pet 1:10–1 Pet 1:12>>] in divers ways of administration,note:proof[<<Heb 1:1–2>>] the whole will of God,note:proof[<<John 15:15>>] in all things concerning their edification and salvation.note:proof[<<Acts 20:32; Eph 4:11–13; John 20:31>>]

@ How doth Christ execute the office of a priest?

Christ executeth the office of a priest, in his once offering himself a sacrifice without spot to God,note:proof[<<Heb 9:14; Heb 9:28>>] to be a reconciliation for the sins of his people;note:proof[<<Heb 2:17>>] and in making continual intercession for them.note:proof[<<Heb 7:25>>]

@ How doth Christ execute the office of a king?

Christ executeth the office of a king, in calling out of the world a people to himself,note:proof[<<Acts 15:14–16; Isa 4:4–5; Gen 49:10; Ps 110:3>>] and giving them officers,note:proof[<<Eph 4:11–12; 1 Cor 12:28>>] laws,note:proof[<<Isa 33:22>>] and censures, by which he visibly governs them;note:proof[<<Matt 18:17–18; 1 Cor 5:4–1 Cor 5:5>>] in bestowing saving grace upon his elect,note:proof[<<Acts 5:31>>] rewarding their obedience,note:proof[<<Rev 2:10; Rev 22:12>>] and correcting them for their sins,note:proof[<<Rev 3:19>>] preserving and supporting them under all their temptations and sufferings,note:proof[<<Isa 63:9>>] restraining and overcoming all their enemies,note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:25; Ps 110:1–2>>] and powerfully ordering all things for his own glory,note:proof[<<Rom 14:10–11>>] and their good;note:proof[<<Rom 8:28>>] and also in taking vengeance on the rest, who know not God, and obey not the gospel.note:proof[<<2 Thess 1:8–2 Thess 1:9; Ps 2:8–9>>]

@ What was the estate of Christ's humiliation?

The estate of Christ's humiliation was that low condition, wherein he for our sakes, emptying himself of his glory, took upon him the form of a servant, in his conception and birth, life, death, and after his death, until his resurrection. note:proof[<<Phil 2:6–8; Luke 1:31; 2 Cor 8:9; Acts 2:24>>]

@ How did Christ humble himself in his conception and birth?

Christ humbled himself in his conception and birth, in that, being from all eternity the Son of God, in the bosom of the Father, he was pleased in the fulness of time to become the son of man, made of a woman of low estate, and to be born of her; with divers circumstances of more than ordinary abasement.note:proof[<<John 1:14; John 1:18; Gal 4:4; Luke 2:7>>]

@ How did Christ humble himself in his life?

Christ humbled himself in his life, by subjecting himself to the law,note:proof[<<Gal 4:4>>] which he perfectly fulfilled;note:proof[<<Matt 5:17; Rom 5:19>>] and by conflicting with the indignities of the world,note:proof[<<Ps 22:6; Heb 12:2–3>>] temptations of Satan,note:proof[<<Matt 4:1–12; Luke 4:13>>] and infirmities in his flesh, whether common to the nature of man, or particularly accompanying that his low condition.note:proof[<<Heb 2:17–18; Heb 4:15; Isa 52:13–14>>]

@ How did Christ humble himself in his death?

Christ humbled himself in his death, in that having been betrayed by Judas,note:proof[<<Matt 27:4>>] forsaken by his disciples,note:proof[<<Matt 26:56>>] scorned and rejected by the world,note:proof[<<Isa 53:2–3>>] condemned by Pilate, and tormented by his persecutors;note:proof[<<Matt 27:26–50; John 19:34>>] having also conflicted with the terrors of death, and the powers of darkness, felt and borne the weight of God's wrath,note:proof[<<Luke 22:44; Matt 27:46>>] he laid down his life an offering for sin,note:proof[<<Isa 53:10>>] enduring the painful, shameful, and cursed death of the cross.note:proof[<<Phil 2:8; Heb 12:2; Gal 3:13>>]

@ Wherein consisted Christ's humiliation after his death?

Christ's humiliation after his death consisted in his being buried,note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:3–1 Cor 15:4>>] and continuing in the state of the dead, and under the power of death till the third day;note:proof[<<Ps 16:10; Acts 2:24–27; Acts 2:31; Rom 6:9; Matt 12:40>>] which hath been otherwise expressed in these words, he descended into hell.

@ What was the estate of Christ's exaltation?

The estate of Christ's exaltation comprehendeth his resurrection,note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:4>>] ascension,note:proof[<<Mark 16:19>>] sitting at the right hand of the Father,note:proof[<<Eph 1:20>>] and his coming again to judge the world.note:proof[<<Acts 1:11; Acts 17:31>>]

@ How was Christ exalted in his resurrection?

Christ was exalted in his resurrection, in that, not having seen corruption in death (of which it was not possible for him to be held),note:proof[<<Acts 2:24; Acts 2:27>>] and having the very same body in which he suffered, with the essential properties thereof note:proof[<<Luke 24:39>>] (but without mortality, and other common infirmities belonging to this life), really united to his soul,note:proof[<<Rom 6:9; Rev 1:18>>] he rose again from the dead the third day by his own power;note:proof[<<John 10:18>>] whereby he declared himself to be the Son of God,note:proof[<<Rom 1:4>>] to have satisfied divine justice,note:proof[<<Rom 8:34>>] to have vanquished death, and him that had the power of it,note:proof[<<Heb 2:14>>] and to be Lord of quick and dead:note:proof[<<Rom 14:9>>] all which he did as a public person,note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:21–1 Cor 15:22>>] the head of his church,note:proof[<<Eph 1:20; Eph 1:22–23; Col 1:18>>] for their justification,note:proof[<<Rom 4:25>>] quickening in grace,note:proof[<<Eph 2:1; Eph 2:5–6; Col 2:12>>] support against enemies,note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:25–1 Cor 15:27>>] and to assure them of their resurrection from the dead at the last day.note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:20>>]

@ How was Christ exalted in his ascension?

Christ was exalted in his ascension, in that having after his resurrection often appeared unto and conversed with his apostles, speaking to them of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God,note:proof[<<Acts 1:2–3>>] and giving them commission to preach the gospel to all nations,note:proof[<<Matt 28:19–20>>] forty days after his resurrection, he, in our nature, and as our head,note:proof[<<Heb 6:20>>] triumphing over enemies,note:proof[<<Eph 4:8>>] visibly went up into the highest heavens, there to receive gifts for men,note:proof[<<Acts 1:9–11; Eph 4:10; Ps 68:18>>] to raise up our affections thither,note:proof[<<Col 3:1–2>>] and to prepare a place for us,note:proof[<<John 14:3>>] where himself is, and shall continue till his second coming at the end of the world.note:proof[<<Acts 3:21>>]

@ How is Christ exalted in his sitting at the right hand of God?

Christ is exalted in his sitting at the right hand of God, in that as God-man he is advanced to the highest favor with God the Father,note:proof[<<Phil 2:9>>] with all fulness of joy,note:proof[<<Acts 2:28; Ps 16:11>>] glory,note:proof[<<John 17:5>>] and power over all things in heaven and earth;note:proof[<<Eph 1:22; 1 Pet 3:22>>] and doth gather and defend his church, and subdue their enemies; furnisheth his ministers and people with gifts and graces,note:proof[<<Eph 4:10–12; Ps 110:1>>] and maketh intercession for them.note:proof[<<Rom 8:34>>]

@ How doth Christ make intercession?

Christ maketh intercession, by his appearing in our nature continually before the Father in heaven,note:proof[<<Heb 9:12; Heb 9:24>>] in the merit of his obedience and sacrifice on earth,note:proof[<<Heb 1:3>>] declaring his will to have it applied to all believers;note:proof[<<John 3:16; John 17:9; John 17:20; John 17:24>>] answering all accusations against them,note:proof[<<Rom 8:33–34>>] and procuring for them quiet of conscience, notwithstanding daily failings,note:proof[<<Rom 5:1–2; 1 John 2:1–1 John 2:2>>] access with boldness to the throne of grace,note:proof[<<Heb 4:16>>] and acceptance of their persons note:proof[<<Eph 1:6>>] and services.note:proof[<<1 Pet 2:5>>]

@ How is Christ to be exalted in his coming again to judge the world?

Christ is to be exalted in his coming again to judge the world, in that he, who was unjustly judged and condemned by wicked men,note:proof[<<Acts 3:14–15>>] shall come again at the last day in great power,note:proof[<<Matt 14:30>>] and in the full manifestation of his own glory, and of his Father's, with all his holy angels,note:proof[<<Luke 9:26; Matt 25:31>>] with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trumpet of God,note:proof[<<1 Thess 4:16>>] to judge the world in righteousness.note:proof[<<Acts 17:31>>]

@ What benefits hath Christ procured by his mediation?

Christ, by his mediation, hath procured redemption,note:proof[<<Heb 9:12>>] with all other benefits of the covenant of grace.note:proof[<<2 Cor 1:20>>]

@ How do we come to be made partakers of the benefits which Christ hath procured?

We are made partakers of the benefits which Christ hath procured,note:proof[<<John 1:11–12>>] by the application of them unto us, which is the work especially of God the Holy Ghost.note:proof[<<Titus 3:5–6>>]

@ Who are made partakers of redemption through Christ?

Redemption is certainly applied, and effectually communicated, to all those for whom Christ hath purchased it;note:proof[<<Eph 1:13–14; John 6:37; John 6:39; John 10:15–16>>] who are in time by the Holy Ghost enabled to believe in Christ according to the gospel.note:proof[<<Eph 2:8; 2 Cor 4:13>>]

@ Can they who have never heard the gospel, and so know not Jesus Christ, nor believe in him, be saved by their living according to the light of nature?

They who, having never heard the gospel,note:proof[<<Rom 10:14>>] know not Jesus Christ,note:proof[<<2 Thess 1:8–2 Thess 1:9; Eph 2:12; John 1:10–12>>] and believe not in him, cannot be saved,note:proof[<<John 8:24; Mark 16:16>>] be they never so diligent to frame their lives according to the light of nature,note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:20–1 Cor 1:24>>] or the laws of that religion which they profess;note:proof[<<John 4:22; Rom 9:31–32; Phil 3:4–9>>] neither is there salvation in any other, but in Christ alone,note:proof[<<Acts 4:12>>] who is the Savior only of his body the church.note:proof[<<Eph 5:23>>]

@ Are all they saved who hear the gospel, and live in the church?

All that hear the gospel, and live in the visible church, are not saved; but they only who are true members of the church invisible.note:proof[<<John 12:38–40; Rom 9:6; Rom 11:7; Matt 7:21; Matt 22:14>>]

@ What is the visible church?

The visible church is a society made up of all such as in all ages and places of the world do profess the true religion,note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:2; 1 Cor 12:13; Rom 15:9–12; Rev 7:9; Ps 2:8; Ps 22:27–31; Ps 45:17; Matt 28:19–20; Isa 59:21>>] and of their children.note:proof[<<1 Cor 7:14; Acts 2:39; Rom 11:16; Gen 17:7>>]

@ What are the special privileges of the visible church?

The visible church hath the privilege of being under God's special care and government;note:proof[<<Isa 4:5–6; 1 Tim 4:10>>] of being protected and preserved in all ages, not withstanding the opposition of all enemies;note:proof[<<Ps 115:1–2; Ps 115:9; Isa 31:4–5; Zech 12:2–4; Zech 12:8–9>>] and of enjoying the communion of saints, the ordinary means of salvation,note:proof[<<Acts 2:39; Acts 2:42>>] and offers of grace by Christ to all the members of it in the ministry of the gospel, testifying, that whosoever believes in him shall be saved,note:proof[<<Ps 147:19–20; Rom 9:4; Eph 4:11–12; Mark 16:15–16>>] and excluding none that will come unto him.note:proof[<<John 6:37>>]

@ What is the invisible church?

The invisible church is the whole number of the elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one under Christ the head.note:proof[<<Eph 1:20; Eph 1:22–23; John 10:16; John 11:52>>]

@ What special benefits do the members of the invisible church enjoy by Christ?

The members of the invisible church by Christ enjoy union and communion with him in grace and glory.note:proof[<<John 17:21; John 17:24; Eph 2:5–6>>]

@ What is that union which the elect have with Christ?

The union which the elect have with Christ is the work of God's grace,note:proof[<<Eph 1:22; Eph 2:6–8>>] whereby they are spiritually and mystically, yet really and inseparably, joined to Christ as their head and husband;note:proof[<<1 Cor 6:17; John 10:28; Eph 5:23; Eph 5:30>>] which is done in their effectual calling.note:proof[<<1 Pet 5:10; 1 Cor 1:9>>]

@ What is effectual calling?

Effectual calling is the work of God's almighty power and grace,note:proof[<<John 5:25; Eph 1:18–20; 2 Tim 1:8–2 Tim 1:9>>] whereby (out of his free and special love to his elect, and from nothing in them moving him thereunto note:proof[<<Titus 3:4–5; Eph 2:4–5; Eph 2:7–9; Rom 9:11>>]) he doth, in his accepted time, invite and draw them to Jesus Christ, by his word and Spirit;note:proof[<<2 Cor 5:20; 2 Cor 6:1–2 Cor 6:2; John 6:44; 2 Thess 2:13–2 Thess 2:14>>] savingly enlightening their minds,note:proof[<<Acts 26:18; 1 Cor 2:10; 1 Cor 2:12>>] renewing and powerfully determining their wills,note:proof[<<Ezek 11:19; Ezek 36:26–27; John 6:45>>] so as they (although in themselves dead in sin) are hereby made willing and able freely to answer his call, and to accept and embrace the grace offered and conveyed therein.note:proof[<<Eph 2:5; Phil 2:13; Deut 30:6>>]

@ Are the elect only effectually called?

All the elect, and they only, are effectually called;note:proof[<<Acts 13:48>>] although others may be, and often are, outwardly called by the ministry of the word,note:proof[<<Matt 22:14>>] and have some common operations of the Spirit;note:proof[<<Matt 7:22; Matt 13:20–21; Heb 6:4–6>>] who, for their wilful neglect and contempt of the grace offered to them, being justly left in their unbelief, do never truly come to Jesus Christ.note:proof[<<John 6:64–65; John 12:38–40; Acts 18:25–27; Ps 81:11–12>>]

@ What is the communion in grace which the members of the invisible church have with Christ?

The communion in grace which the members of the invisible church have with Christ, is their partaking of the virtue of his mediation, in their justification,note:proof[<<Rom 8:30>>] adoption,note:proof[<<Eph 1:5>>] sanctification, and whatever else, in this life, manifests their union with him.note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:30>>]

@ What is justification?

Justification is an act of God's free grace unto sinners,note:proof[<<Rom 3:22; Rom 3:24–25; Rom 4:5>>] in which he pardoneth all their sins, accepteth and accounteth their persons righteous in his sight;note:proof[<<2 Cor 5:19; 2 Cor 5:21; Rom 3:22–25; Rom 3:27–28>>] not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them,note:proof[<<Titus 3:5; Titus 3:7; Eph 1:7>>] but only for the perfect obedience and full satisfaction of Christ, by God imputed to them,note:proof[<<Rom 4:6–8; Rom 5:17–19>>] and received by faith alone.note:proof[<<Acts 10:43; Gal 2:16; Phil 3:9>>]

@ How is justification an act of God's free grace?

Although Christ, by his obedience and death, did make a proper, real, and full satisfaction to God's justice in the behalf of them that are justified;note:proof[<<Rom 5:8–10; Rom 5:19>>] yet inasmuch as God accepteth the satisfaction from a surety, which he might have demanded of them, and did provide this surety, his own only Son,note:proof[<<2 Tim 2:5–2 Tim 2:6; Heb 7:22; Heb 10:10; Matt 20:28; Dan 9:24; Dan 9:26; Isa 53:4–6; Isa 53:10–12; Rom 8:32; 1 Pet 1:18–1 Pet 1:19>>] imputing his righteousness to them,note:proof[<<2 Cor 5:21>>] and requiring nothing of them for their justification but faith,note:proof[<<Rom 3:24–25>>] which also is his gift,note:proof[<<Eph 2:8>>] their justification is to them of free grace.note:proof[<<Eph 1:7>>]

@ What is justifying faith?

Justifying faith is a saving grace,note:proof[<<Heb 10:39>>] wrought in the heart of a sinner by the Spirit note:proof[<<2 Cor 4:13; Eph 1:17–19>>] and word of God,note:proof[<<Rom 10:14; Rom 10:17>>] whereby he, being convinced of his sin and misery, and of the disability in himself and all other creatures to recover him out of his lost condition,note:proof[<<Acts 2:37; Acts 4:12; Acts 16:30; John 16:8–9; Rom 5:6; Eph 2:1>>] not only assenteth to the truth of the promise of the gospel,note:proof[<<Eph 1:13>>] but receiveth and resteth upon Christ and his righteousness, therein held forth, for pardon of sin,note:proof[<<John 1:12; Acts 10:43; Acts 16:31>>] and for the accepting and accounting of his person righteous in the sight of God for salvation.note:proof[<<Phil 3:9; Acts 15:11>>]

@ How doth faith justify a sinner in the sight of God?

Faith justifies a sinner in the sight of God, not because of those other graces which do always accompany it, or of good works that are the fruits of it,note:proof[<<Gal 3:11; Rom 3:28>>] nor as if the grace of faith, or any act thereof, were imputed to him for his justification;note:proof[<<Rom 4:5; Rom 10:10>>] but only as it is an instrument by which he receiveth and applies Christ and his righteousness.note:proof[<<John 1:12; Phil 3:9; Gal 2:16>>]

@ What is adoption?

Adoption is an act of the free grace of God,note:proof[<<1 John 3:1>>] in and for his only Son Jesus Christ,note:proof[<<Eph 1:5; Gal 4:4–5>>] whereby all those that are justified are received into the number of his children,note:proof[<<John 1:12>>] have his name put upon them,note:proof[<<2 Cor 4:18; Rev 3:12>>] the Spirit of his Son given to them,note:proof[<<Gal 4:6>>] are under his fatherly care and dispensations,note:proof[<<Ps 103:13; Prov 14:26; Matt 6:32>>] admitted to all the liberties and privileges of the sons of God, made heirs of all the promises, and fellow heirs with Christ in glory.note:proof[<<Heb 6:12; Rom 8:17>>]

@ What is sanctification?

Sanctification is a work of God's grace, whereby they whom God hath, before the foundation of the world, chosen to be holy, are in time, through the powerful operation of his Spirit note:proof[<<Eph 1:4; 1 Cor 6:11; 2 Thess 2:13>>] applying the death and resurrection of Christ unto them,note:proof[<<Rom 6:4–6>>] renewed in their whole man after the image of God;note:proof[<<Eph 4:23–24>>] having the seeds of repentance unto life, and all other saving graces, put into their hearts,note:proof[<<Acts 11:18; 1 John 3:9>>] and those graces so stirred up, increased, and strengthened,note:proof[<<Jude 1:20; Heb 6:11–12; Eph 3:16–19; Col 1:10–11>>] as that they more and more die unto sin, and rise unto newness of life.note:proof[<<Rom 6:4; Rom 6:14; Gal 5:24>>]

@ What is repentance unto life?

Repentance unto life is a saving grace,note:proof[<<2 Tim 2:25>>] wrought in the heart of a sinner by the Spirit note:proof[<<Zech 12:10>>] and word of God,note:proof[<<Acts 11:18; Acts 11:20–21>>] whereby, out of the sight and sense, not only of the danger,note:proof[<<Ezek 18:28; Ezek 18:30; Ezek 18:32; Luke 15:17–18; Hos 2:6–7>>] but also of the filthiness and odiousness of his sins,note:proof[<<Ezek 36:31; Isa 30:22>>] and upon the apprehension of God's mercy in Christ to such as are penitent,note:proof[<<Joel 2:12–13>>] he so grieves for note:proof[<<Jer 31:18–19>>] and hates his sins,note:proof[<<2 Cor 7:11>>] as that he turns from them all to God,note:proof[<<Acts 26:18; Ezek 14:6; 1 Kgs 8:47–1 Kgs 8:48>>] purposing and endeavoring constantly to walk with him in all the ways of new obedience.note:proof[<<Ps 119:6; Ps 119:59; Ps 119:128; Luke 1:6; 2 Kgs 23:25>>]

@ Wherein do justification and sanctification differ?

Although sanctification be inseparably joined with justification,note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:30; 1 Cor 6:11>>] yet they differ, in that God in justification imputeth the righteousness of Christ;note:proof[<<Rom 4:6; Rom 4:8>>] in sanctification his Spirit infuseth grace, and enableth to the exercise thereof;note:proof[<<Ezek 36:27>>] in the former, sin is pardoned;note:proof[<<Rom 3:24–25>>] in the other, it is subdued:note:proof[<<Rom 6:6; Rom 6:14>>] the one doth equally free all believers from the revenging wrath of God, and that perfectly in this life, that they never fall into condemnation;note:proof[<<Rom 8:33–34>>] the other is neither equal in all,note:proof[<<1 John 2:12–1 John 2:14; Heb 5:12–14>>] nor in this life perfect in any,note:proof[<<1 John 1:8; 1 John 1:10>>] but growing up to perfection.note:proof[<<2 Cor 7:1; Phil 3:12–14>>]

@ Whence ariseth the imperfection of sanctification in believers?

The imperfection of sanctification in believers ariseth from the remnants of sin abiding in every part of them, and the perpetual lustings of the flesh against the spirit; whereby they are often foiled with temptations, and fall into many sins,note:proof[<<Rom 7:18; Rom 7:23; Mark 14:66–72; Gal 2:11–12>>] are hindered in all their spiritual services,note:proof[<<Heb 12:1>>] and their best works are imperfect and defiled in the sight of God.note:proof[<<Isa 64:6; Exod 28:38>>]

@ May not true believers, by reason of their imperfections, and the many temptations and sins they are overtaken with, fall away from the state of grace ?

True believers, by reason of the unchangeable love of God,note:proof[<<Jer 31:3>>] and his decree and covenant to give them perseverance,note:proof[<<2 Tim 2:19–2 Tim 2:21; 2 Sam 23:5>>] their inseparable union with Christ,note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:8–1 Cor 1:9>>] his continual intercession for them,note:proof[<<Heb 7:25; Luke 22:32>>] and the Spirit and seed of God abiding in them,note:proof[<<1 John 2:27; 1 John 3:9>>] can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace,note:proof[<<Jer 32:40; John 10:28>>] but are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation.note:proof[<<1 Pet 1:5>>]

@ Can true believers be infallibly assured that they are in the estate of grace, and that they shall persevere therein unto salvation?

Such as truly believe in Christ, and endeavor to walk in all good conscience before him,note:proof[<<1 John 2:3>>] may, without extraordinary revelation, by faith grounded upon the truth of God's promises, and by the Spirit enabling them to discern in themselves those graces to which the promises of life are made,note:proof[<<1 Cor 2:12; 1 John 3:14; 1 John 3:18–1 John 3:19; 1 John 3:21; 1 John 3:24; 1 John 4:13; 1 John 4:16; Heb 6:11–12>>] and bearing witness with their spirits that they are the children of God,note:proof[<<Rom 8:16>>] be infallibly assured that they are in the estate of grace, and shall persevere therein unto salvation.note:proof[<<1 John 5:13>>]

@ Are all true believers at all times assured of their present being in the estate of grace, and that they shall be saved?

Assurance of grace and salvation not being of the essence of faith,note:proof[<<Eph 1:13>>] true believers may wait long before they obtain it;note:proof[<<Isa 50:10; Ps 88>>] and, after the enjoyment thereof, may have it weakened and intermitted, through manifold distempers, sins, temptations, and desertions;note:proof[<<Ps 22:1; Ps 31:22; Ps 51:8; Ps 51:12; Ps 77:1–12; Song 5:2–3; Song 5:6>>] yet are they never left without such a presence and support of the Spirit of God as keeps them from sinking into utter despair.note:proof[<<1 John 3:9; Job 13:15; Ps 73:15; Ps 73:23; Isa 54:7–10>>]

@ What is the communion in glory which the members of the invisible church have with Christ?

The communion in glory which the members of the invisible church have with Christ, is in this life,note:proof[<<2 Cor 3:18>>] immediately after death,note:proof[<<Luke 23:43>>] and at last perfected at the resurrection and day of judgment.note:proof[<<1 Thess 4:17>>]

@ What is the communion in glory with Christ which the members of the invisible church enjoy in this life?

The members of the invisible church have communicated to them in this life the firstfruits of glory with Christ, as they are members of him their head, and so in him are interested in that glory which he is fully possessed of;note:proof[<<Eph 2:5>>] and, as an earnest thereof, enjoy the sense of God's love,note:proof[<<Rom 5:5; 2 Cor 1:22>>] peace of conscience, joy in the Holy Ghost, and hope of glory;note:proof[<<Rom 5:1–2; Rom 14:17>>] as, on the contrary, sense of God's revenging wrath, horror of conscience, and a fearful expectation of judgment, are to the wicked the beginning of their torments which they shall endure after death.note:proof[<<Gen 4:13; Matt 27:4; Heb 10:27; Rom 2:9; Mark 9:44>>]

@ Shall all men die?

Death being threatened as the wages of sin,note:proof[<<Rom 6:23>>] it is appointed unto all men once to die;note:proof[<<Heb 9:27>>] for that all have sinned.note:proof[<<Rom 5:12>>]

@ Death, being the wages of sin, why are not the righteous delivered from death, seeing all their sins are forgiven in Christ?

The righteous shall be delivered from death itself at the last day, and even in death are delivered from the sting and curse of it;note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:26; 1 Cor 15:55–1 Cor 15:57; Heb 2:15>>] so that, although they die, yet it is out of God's love,note:proof[<<Isa 57:1–2; 2 Kgs 22:20>>] to free them perfectly from sin and misery,note:proof[<<Rev 14:13; Eph 5:27>>] and to make them capable of further communion with Christ in glory, which they then enter upon.note:proof[<<Luke 23:43; Phil 1:23>>]

@ What is the communion in glory with Christ, which the members of the invisible church enjoy immediately after death ?

The communion in glory with Christ, which the members of the invisible church enjoy immediately after death, is, in that their souls are then made perfect in holiness,note:proof[<<Heb 12:23>>] and received into the highest heavens,note:proof[<<2 Cor 5:1; 2 Cor 5:6; 2 Cor 5:8; Phil 1:23; Acts 3:21; Eph 4:10>>] where they behold the face of God in light and glory,note:proof[<<1 John 3:2; 1 Cor 13:12>>] waiting for the full redemption of their bodies,note:proof[<<Rom 8:23; Ps 16:9>>] which even in death continue united to Christ,note:proof[<<1 Thess 4:14>>] and rest in their graves as in their beds,note:proof[<<Isa 57:2>>] till at the last day they be again united to their souls.note:proof[<<Job 19:26–27>>] Whereas the souls of the wicked are at their death cast into hell, where they remain in torments and utter darkness, and their bodies kept in their graves, as in their prisons, till the resurrection and judgment of the great day.note:proof[<<Luke 16:23–24; Acts 1:25; Jude 1:6–7>>]

@ What are we to believe concerning the resurrection?

We are to believe, that at the last day there shall be a general resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust:note:proof[<<Acts 24:15>>] when they that are then found alive shall in a moment be changed; and the selfsame bodies of the dead which were laid in the grave, being then again united to their souls forever, shall be raised up by the power of Christ.note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:51–1 Cor 15:53; 1 Thess 4:15–1 Thess 4:17; John 5:28–29>>] The bodies of the just, by the Spirit of Christ, and by virtue of his resurrection as their head, shall be raised in power, spiritual, incorruptible, and made like to his glorious body;note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:21–1 Cor 15:23; 1 Cor 15:42–1 Cor 15:44; Phil 3:21>>] and the bodies of the wicked shall be raised up in dishonor by him, as an offended judge.note:proof[<<John 5:27–29; Matt 25:33>>]

@ What shall immediately follow after the resurrection?

Immediately after the resurrection shall follow the general and final judgment of angels and men;note:proof[<<2 Pet 2:4; Jude 1:6–7; Jude 1:14–15; Matt 25:46>>] the day and hour whereof no man knows, that all may watch and pray, and be ever ready for the coming of the Lord.note:proof[<<Matt 24:36; Matt 24:42; Matt 24:44; Luke 21:35–36>>]

@ What shall be done to the wicked at the day of judgment?

At the day of judgment, the wicked shall be set on Christ's left hand,note:proof[<<Matt 25:33>>] and, upon clear evidence, and full conviction of their own consciences,note:proof[<<Rom 2:15–16>>] shall have the fearful but just sentence of condemnation pronounced against them;note:proof[<<Matt 25:41–43>>] and thereupon shall be cast out from the favorable presence of God, and the glorious fellowship with Christ, his saints, and all his holy angels, into hell, to be punished with unspeakable torments, both of body and soul, with the devil and his angels forever.note:proof[<<Luke 16:26; 2 Thess 1:8–2 Thess 1:9>>]

@ What shall be done to the righteous at the day of judgment?

At the day of judgment, the righteous, being caught up to Christ in the clouds,note:proof[<<1 Thess 4:17>>] shall be set on his right hand, and there openly acknowledged and acquitted,note:proof[<<Matt 10:32; Matt 25:33>>] shall join with him in the judging of reprobate angels and men,note:proof[<<1 Cor 6:2–1 Cor 6:3>>] and shall be received into heaven,note:proof[<<Matt 25:34; Matt 25:46>>] where they shall be fully and forever freed from all sin and misery;note:proof[<<Eph 5:27; Rev 14:13>>] filled with inconceivable joys,note:proof[<<Ps 16:11>>] made perfectly holy and happy both in body and soul, in the company of innumerable saints and holy angels,note:proof[<<Heb 12:22–23>>] but especially in the immediate vision and fruition of God the Father, of our Lord Jesus Christ, and of the Holy Spirit, to all eternity.note:proof[<<1 John 3:2; 1 Cor 13:12; 1 Thess 4:17–1 Thess 4:18>>] And this is the perfect and full communion, which the members of the invisible church shall enjoy with Christ in glory, at the resurrection and day of judgment.

@ What is the duty which God requireth of man?

The duty which God requireth of man, is obedience to his revealed will.note:proof[<<Rom 12:1–2; Mic 6:8; 1 Sam 15:22>>]

@ What did God at first reveal unto man as the rule of his obedience?

The rule of obedience revealed to Adam in the estate of innocence, and to all mankind in him, besides a special command not to eat of the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, was the moral law.note:proof[<<Gen 1:26–27; Gen 2:17; Rom 2:14–15; Rom 10:5>>]

@ What is the moral law?

The moral law is the declaration of the will of God to mankind, directing and binding everyone to personal, perfect, and perpetual conformity and obedience thereunto, in the frame and disposition of the whole man, soul and body,note:proof[<<Deut 5:1–3; Deut 5:31; Deut 5:33; Luke 10:26–27; Gal 3:10; 1 Thess 5:23>>] and in performance of all those duties of holiness and righteousness which he oweth to God and man:note:proof[<<Luke 1:75; Acts 14:16>>] promising life upon the fulfilling, and threatening death upon the breach of it.note:proof[<<Rom 10:5; Gal 3:10; Gal 3:12>>]

@ Is there any use of the moral law to man since the fall?

Although no man, since the fall, can attain to righteousness and life by the moral law;note:proof[<<Rom 8:3; Gal 2:16>>] yet there is great use thereof, as well common to all men, as peculiar either to the unregenerate, or the regenerate.note:proof[<<1 Tim 1:8>>]

@ Of what use is the moral law to all men?

The moral law is of use to all men, to inform them of the holy nature and will of God,note:proof[<<Lev 11:44–45; Lev 20:7–8; Rom 7:12>>] and of their duty, binding them to walk accordingly;note:proof[<<Mic 6:8; Jas 2:10–11>>] to convince them of their disability to keep it, and of the sinful pollution of their nature, hearts, and lives;note:proof[<<Ps 19:11–12; Rom 3:20; Rom 7:7>>] to humble them in the sense of their sin and misery,note:proof[<<Rom 3:9; Rom 3:23>>] and thereby help them to a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ,note:proof[<<Gal 3:21–22>>] and of the perfection of his obedience.note:proof[<<Rom 10:4>>]

@ What particular use is there of the moral law to unregenerate men?

The moral law is of use to unregenerate men, to awaken their consciences to flee from wrath to come,note:proof[<<1 Tim 1:9–1 Tim 1:10>>] and to drive them to Christ;note:proof[<<Gal 3:24>>] or, upon their continuance in the estate and way of sin, to leave them inexcusable,note:proof[<<Rom 1:20; Rom 2:15>>] and under the curse thereof.note:proof[<<Gal 3:10>>]

@ What special use is there of the moral law to the regenerate?

Although they that are regenerate, and believe in Christ, be delivered from the moral law as a covenant of works,note:proof[<<Rom 6:14; Rom 7:4; Rom 7:6; Gal 4:4–5>>] so as thereby they are neither justified note:proof[<<Rom 3:20>>] nor condemned;note:proof[<<Gal 5:23; Rom 8:1>>] yet, besides the general uses thereof common to them with all men, it is of special use, to show them how much they are bound to Christ for his fulfilling it, and enduring the curse thereof in their stead, and for their good;note:proof[<<Rom 7:24–25; Rom 8:3–4; Gal 3:13–14>>] and thereby to provoke them to more thankfulness,note:proof[<<Luke 1:68–69; Luke 1:74–75; Col 1:12–14>>] and to express the same in their greater care to conform themselves thereunto as the rule of their obedience.note:proof[<<Rom 7:22; Rom 12:2; Titus 2:11–14>>]

@ Where is the moral law summarily comprehended?

The moral law is summarily comprehended in the ten commandments, which were delivered by the voice of God upon mount Sinai, and written by him in two tables of stone;note:proof[<<Deut 10:4; Exod 34:1–4>>] and are recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus. The four first commandments containing our duty to God, and the other six our duty to man.note:proof[<<Matt 22:37–38; Matt 22:40>>]

@ What rules are to be observed for the right understanding of the ten commandments?

For the right understanding of the ten commandments, these rules are to be observed:
1. That the law is perfect, and bindeth everyone to full conformity in the whole man unto the righteousness thereof, and unto entire obedience forever; so as to require the utmost perfection of every duty, and to forbid the least degree of every sin.note:proof[<<Ps 19:7; Jas 2:10; Matt 5:21–22>>]
2. That it is spiritual, and so reaches the understanding, will, affections, and all other powers of the soul; as well as words, works, and gestures.note:proof[<<Rom 7:14; Deut 6:5; Matt 5:21–22; Matt 5:27–28; Matt 5:33–34; Matt 5:37–39; Matt 5:43–44; Matt 22:37–39>>]
3. That one and the same thing, in divers respects, is required or forbidden in several commandments.note:proof[<<Col 3:5; Amos 8:5; Prov 1:19; 1 Tim 6:1>>]
4. That as, where a duty is commanded, the contrary sin is forbidden;note:proof[<<Isa 58:13; Deut 6:13; Matt 4:9–10; Matt 15:4–6>>] and, where a sin is forbidden, the contrary duty is commanded:note:proof[<<Matt 5:21–25; Eph 4:28>>] so, where a promise is annexed, the contrary threatening is included;note:proof[<<Exod 20:12; Prov 30:17>>] and, where a threatening is annexed, the contrary promise is included.note:proof[<<Jer 18:7–8; Exod 20:7; Ps 15:1; Ps 15:4–5; Ps 24:4–5>>]
5. That what God forbids, is at no time to be done;note:proof[<<Job 13:7; Job 36:21; Rom 3:8; Heb 11:25>>] What he commands, is always our duty;note:proof[<<Deut 4:8–9>>] and yet every particular duty is not to be done at all times.note:proof[<<Matt 12:7>>]
6. That under one sin or duty, all of the same kind are forbidden or commanded; together with all the causes, means, occasions, and appearances thereof, and provocations thereunto.note:proof[<<Matt 5:21–22; Matt 5:27–28; Matt 15:4–6; Heb 10:24–25; 1 Thess 5:22–1 Thess 5:23; Gal 5:26; Col 3:21>>]
7. That what is forbidden or commanded to ourselves, we are bound, according to our places, to endeavor that it may be avoided or performed by others, according to the duty of their places.note:proof[<<Exod 20:10; Lev 19:17; Gen 18:19; Josh 24:15; Deut 6:6–7>>]
8. That in what is commanded to others, we are bound, according to our places and callings, to be helpful to them;note:proof[<<2 Cor 1:24>>] and to take heed of partaking with others in: What is forbidden them.note:proof[<<1 Tim 5:22>>]

@ Which is the fourth commandment?

The fourth commandment is, Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. @ What is required in the fourth commandment?

The fourth commandment requires of all men the sanctifying or keeping holy to God such set times as he hath appointed in his word, expressly one whole day in seven; which was the seventh from the beginning of the world to the resurrection of Christ, and the first day of the week ever since, and so to continue to the end of the world; which is the Christian sabbath,note:proof[<<Deut 5:12; Deut 5:14; Deut 5:18; Gen 2:2–3; 1 Cor 16:1–1 Cor 16:2; Acts 20:7; Matt 5:17–18; Isa 56:2; Isa 56:4; Isa 56:6–7>>] and in the New Testament called The Lord's day.note:proof[<<Rev 1:10>>]

@ How is the sabbath or the Lord's day to be sanctified?

The sabbath or Lord's day is to be sanctified by an holy resting all the day,note:proof[<<Exod 20:8; Exod 20:10>>] not only from such works as are at all times sinful, but even from such worldly employments and recreations as are on other days lawful;note:proof[<<Exod 16:25–28; Neh 13:15–22; Jer 17:21–22>>] and making it our delight to spend the whole time (except so much of it as is to betaken up in works of necessity and mercy)note:proof[<<Matt 12:1–13>>] in the public and private exercises of God's worship:note:proof[<<Isa 58:13–14; Isa 66:23; Luke 4:16; Acts 20:7; 1 Cor 16:1–1 Cor 16:2; Ps 92; Lev 23:3>>] and, to that end, we are to prepare our hearts, and with such foresight, diligence, and moderation, to dispose and seasonably dispatch our worldly business, that we may be the more free and fit for the duties of that day.note:proof[<<Exod 16:22; Exod 16:25–26; Exod 16:29; Exod 20:8; Luke 23:54; Luke 23:56; Neh 13:19>>]

The charge of keeping the sabbath is more specially directed to governors of families, and other superiors, because they are bound not only to keep it themselves, but to see that it be observed by all those that are under their charge; and because they are prone ofttimes to hinder them by employments of their own.note:proof[<<Exod 20:10; Exod 23:12; Josh 24:15; Neh 13:15; Neh 13:17; Jer 17:20–22>>]

The sins forbidden in the fourth commandment are, all omissions of the duties required,note:proof[<<Ezek 22:26>>] all careless, negligent, and unprofitable performing of them, and being weary of them;note:proof[<<Acts 15:7; Acts 15:9; Ezek 33:30–32; Amos 8:5; Mal 1:13>>] all profaning the day by idleness, and doing that which is in itself sinful;note:proof[<<Ezek 23:38>>] and by all needless works, words, and thoughts, about our worldly employments and recreations.note:proof[<<Jer 17:24; Jer 17:27; Isa 58:13>>]

The reasons annexed to the fourth commandment, the more to enforce it, are taken from the equity of it, God allowing us six days of seven for our own affairs, and reserving but one for himself, in these words, Six days shalt thou labor, and do all thy work:note:proof[<<Exod 20:9>>] from God's challenging a special propriety in that day, The seventh day is the sabbath of the Lord thy God:note:proof[<<Exod 20:10>>] from the example of God, who in six days made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: and from that blessing which God put upon that day, not only in sanctifying it to be a day for his service, but in ordaining it to be a means of blessing to us in our sanctifying it; Wherefore the Lord blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.note:proof[<<Exod 20:11>>]

All transgressions of the law of God are not equally heinous; but some sins in themselves, and by reason of several aggravations, are more heinous in the sight of God than others.note:proof[<<John 19:11; Ezek 8:6; Ezek 8:13; Ezek 8:15; 1 John 5:16; Ps 78:17; Ps 78:32; Ps 78:56>>]

@ What are those aggravations that make some sins more heinous than others?

Sins receive their aggravations,

From the persons offending:note:proof[<<Jer 2:8>>] if they be of riper age,note:proof[<<Job 32:7; Job 32:9; Eccl 4:13>>] greater experience or grace,note:proof[<<1 Kgs 11:4; 1 Kgs 11:9>>] eminent for profession,note:proof[<<2 Sam 12:14; 1 Cor 5:1>>] gifts,note:proof[<<Jas 4:17; Luke 12:47–48>>] place,note:proof[<<Jer 5:4–5>>] office,note:proof[<<2 Sam 12:7–2 Sam 12:9; Ezek 8:11–12>>] guides to others,note:proof[<<Rom 2:17–24>>] and whose example is likely to be followed by others.note:proof[<<Gal 2:11–14>>]

From the nature and quality of the offense:note:proof[<<Prov 6:30–35>>] if it be against the express letter of the law,note:proof[<<Ezra 9:10–12; 1 Kgs 11:9–1 Kgs 11:10>>] break many commandments, contain in it many sins:note:proof[<<Col 3:5; 1 Tim 6:1; Prov 5:8–12; Prov 6:32–33; Josh 7:21>>] if not only conceived in the heart, but breaks forth in words and actions,note:proof[<<Jas 1:14–15; Matt 5:22; Mic 2:1>>] scandalize others,note:proof[<<Matt 18:7; Rom 2:23–24>>] and admit of no reparation:note:proof[<<Deut 22:22; Deut 22:28–29; Prov 6:32–35>>] if against means,note:proof[<<Matt 11:21–24; John 15:22>>] mercies,note:proof[<<Isa 1:3; Deut 32:6>>] judgments,note:proof[<<Amos 4:8–11; Jer 5:8>>] light of nature,note:proof[<<Rom 1:26–27>>] conviction of conscience,note:proof[<<Rom 1:32; Dan 5:22; Titus 3:10–11>>] public or private admonition,note:proof[<<Prov 29:1>>] censures of the church,note:proof[<<Titus 3:10; Matt 18:17>>] civil punishments;note:proof[<<Prov 23:35; Prov 27:22>>] and our prayers, purposes, promises,note:proof[<<Ps 78:34–37; Jer 2:20; Jer 13:5–6; Jer 13:20–21>>] vows,note:proof[<<Eccl 5:4–6; Prov 20:25>>] covenants,note:proof[<<Lev 26:25>>] and engagements to God or men:note:proof[<<Prov 2:17; Ezek 17:18–19>>] if done deliberately,note:proof[<<Ps 36:4>>] wilfully,note:proof[<<Jer 6:16>>] presumptuously,note:proof[<<Num 15:30; Exod 21:14>>] impudently,note:proof[<<Jer 3:3; Prov 7:13>>] boastingly,note:proof[<<Ps 52:1>>] maliciously,note:proof[<<3 John 1:10>>] frequently,note:proof[<<Num 14:22>>] obstinately,note:proof[<<Zech 7:11–12>>] with delight,note:proof[<<Prov 2:14>>] continuance,note:proof[<<Isa 57:17>>] or relapsing after repentance.note:proof[<<Jer 34:8–11; 2 Pet 2:20–2 Pet 2:22>>]

From circumstances of time note:proof[<<2 Kgs 5:26>>] and place:note:proof[<<Jer 7:10; Isa 26:10>>] if on the Lord's day,note:proof[<<Ezek 23:37–39>>] or other times of divine worship;note:proof[<<Isa 58:3–5; Num 25:6–7>>] or immediately before note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:20–1 Cor 11:21>>] or after these,note:proof[<<Jer 7:8–10; Jer 7:14–15; John 13:27; John 13:30>>] or other helps to prevent or remedy such miscarriages:note:proof[<<Ezra 9:13–14>>] if in public, or in the presence of others, who are thereby likely to be provoked or defiled.note:proof[<<2 Sam 16:22; 1 Sam 2:22–1 Sam 2:24>>]


@ What doth every sin deserve at the hands of God?

Every sin, even the least, being against the sovereignty,note:proof[<<Jas 2:10–11>>] goodness,note:proof[<<Exod 20:1–2>>] and holiness of God,note:proof[<<Hab 1:13; Lev 10:3; Lev 11:44–45>>] and against his righteous law,note:proof[<<1 John 3:4; Rom 7:12>>] deserveth his wrath and curse,note:proof[<<Eph 5:6; Gal 3:10>>] both in this life,note:proof[<<Lam 3:39; Deut 28:15–68>>] and that which is to come;note:proof[<<Matt 25:41>>] and cannot be expiated but by the blood of Christ.note:proof[<<Heb 9:22; 1 Pet 1:18–1 Pet 1:19>>]

@ What doth God require of us, that we may escape his wrath and curse due to us by reason of the transgression of the law?

That we may escape the wrath and curse of God due to us by reason of the transgression of the law, he requireth of us repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ,note:proof[<<Acts 16:30–31; Acts 20:21; Matt 3:7–8; Luke 13:3; Luke 13:5; John 3:16; John 3:18>>] and the diligent use of the outward means whereby Christ communicates to us the benefits of his mediation.note:proof[<<Prov 2:1–5; Prov 8:33–36>>]

@ What are the outward means whereby Christ communicates to us the benefits of his mediation?

The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicates to his church the benefits of his mediation, are all his ordinances; especially the word, sacraments, and prayer; all which are made effectual to the elect for their salvation.note:proof[<<Matt 28:19–20; Acts 2:42; Acts 2:46–47>>]

@ How is the word made effectual to salvation?

The Spirit of God makes the reading, but especially the preaching of the word, an effectual means of enlightening,note:proof[<<Neh 8:8; Acts 26:18; Ps 19:8>>] convincing, and humbling sinners;note:proof[<<1 Cor 14:24–1 Cor 14:25; 2 Chr 34:18–2 Chr 34:19; 2 Chr 34:26–2 Chr 34:28>>] of driving them out of themselves, and drawing them unto Christ;note:proof[<<Acts 2:37; Acts 2:41; Acts 8:27–39>>] of conforming them to his image,note:proof[<<2 Cor 3:18>>] and subduing them to his will;note:proof[<<2 Cor 10:4–2 Cor 10:6; Rom 6:17>>] of strengthening them against temptations and corruptions;note:proof[<<Matt 4:4; Matt 4:7; Matt 4:10; Eph 6:16–17; Ps 19:11; 1 Cor 10:11>>] of building them up in grace,note:proof[<<Acts 20:32; 2 Tim 3:15–2 Tim 3:17>>] and establishing their hearts in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation.note:proof[<<Rom 1:16; Rom 10:13–17; Rom 15:4; Rom 16:25; 1 Thess 3:2; 1 Thess 3:10–1 Thess 3:11; 1 Thess 3:13>>]

@ Is the word of God to be read by all?

Although all are not to be permitted to read the word publicly to the congregation,note:proof[<<Deut 31:9; Deut 31:11–13; Neh 8:2–3; Neh 9:3–5>>] yet all sorts of people are bound to read it apart by themselves,note:proof[<<Deut 17:19; Rev 1:3; John 5:39; Isa 34:16>>] and with their families:note:proof[<<Deut 6:6–9; Gen 18:17; Gen 18:19; Ps 78:5–7>>] to which end, the holy scriptures are to be translated out of the original into vulgar languages.note:proof[<<1 Cor 14:6; 1 Cor 14:9; 1 Cor 14:11–1 Cor 14:12; 1 Cor 14:15–1 Cor 14:16; 1 Cor 14:24; 1 Cor 14:27–1 Cor 14:28>>]

@ How is the word of God to be read?

The holy scriptures are to be read with an high and reverent esteem of them;note:proof[<<Ps 19:10; Neh 8:3–10; Exod 24:7; 2 Chr 34:27; Isa 66:2>>] with a firm persuasion that they are the very word of God,note:proof[<<2 Pet 1:19–2 Pet 1:21>>] and that he only can enable us to understand them;note:proof[<<Luke 24:45; 2 Cor 3:13–2 Cor 3:16>>] with desire to know, believe, and obey the will of God revealed in them;note:proof[<<Deut 17:10; Deut 17:20>>] with diligence,note:proof[<<Acts 17:11>>] and attention to the matter and scope of them;note:proof[<<Acts 8:30; Acts 8:34; Luke 10:26–28>>] with meditation,note:proof[<<Ps 1:2; Ps 119:97>>] application,note:proof[<<2 Chr 24:21>>] self-denial,note:proof[<<Prov 3:5; Deut 33:3>>] and prayer.note:proof[<<Prov 2:1–6; Ps 119:18; Neh 7:6; Neh 7:8>>]

@ By whom is the word of God to be preached?

The word of God is to be preached only by such as are sufficiently gifted,note:proof[<<1 Tim 3:2; 1 Tim 3:6; Eph 4:8–11; Hos 4:6; Mal 2:7; 2 Cor 3:6>>] and also duly approved and called to that office.note:proof[<<Jer 14:15; Rom 10:15; Heb 5:4; 1 Cor 12:28–1 Cor 12:29; 1 Tim 3:10; 1 Tim 4:14; 1 Tim 5:22>>]

@ How is the word of God to be preached by those that are called thereunto?

They that are called to labor in the ministry of the word, are to preach sound doctrine,note:proof[<<Titus 2:1; Titus 2:8>>] diligently,note:proof[<<Acts 18:25>>] in season and out of season;note:proof[<<2 Tim 4:2>>] plainly,note:proof[<<1 Cor 14:19>>] not in the enticing words of man's wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit, and of power;note:proof[<<1 Cor 2:4>>] faithfully,note:proof[<<Jer 23:28; 1 Cor 4:1–1 Cor 4:2>>] making known the whole counsel of God;note:proof[<<Acts 20:27>>] wisely,note:proof[<<Col 1:28; 2 Tim 2:15>>] applying themselves to the necessities and capacities of the hearers;note:proof[<<1 Cor 3:2; Heb 5:12–14; Luke 12:42>>] zealously,note:proof[<<Acts 18:25>>] with fervent love to God note:proof[<<2 Cor 5:13–2 Cor 5:14; Phil 1:15–17>>] and the souls of his people;note:proof[<<Col 4:12; 2 Cor 12:15>>] sincerely,note:proof[<<2 Cor 2:17; 2 Cor 4:2>>] aiming at his glory,note:proof[<<1 Thess 2:4–1 Thess 2:6; John 7:18>>] and their conversion,note:proof[<<1 Cor 9:19–1 Cor 9:22>>] edification,note:proof[<<2 Cor 12:19; Eph 4:12>>] and salvation.note:proof[<<1 Tim 4:16; Acts 26:16–18>>]

@ What is required of those that hear the word preached?

It is required of those that hear the word preached, that they attend upon it with diligence,note:proof[<<Prov 8:34>>] preparation,note:proof[<<1 Pet 2:1–1 Pet 2:2; Luke 8:18>>] and prayer;note:proof[<<Ps 119:18; Eph 6:18–19>>] examine: What they hear by the scriptures;note:proof[<<Acts 17:11>>] receive the truth with faith,note:proof[<<Heb 4:2>>] love,note:proof[<<2 Thess 2:10>>] meekness,note:proof[<<Jas 1:21>>] and readiness of mind,note:proof[<<Acts 17:11>>] as the word of God;note:proof[<<1 Thess 2:13>>] meditate,note:proof[<<Luke 9:44; Heb 2:1>>] and confer of it;note:proof[<<Luke 24:14; Deut 6:6–7>>] hide it in their hearts,note:proof[<<Prov 2:1; Ps 119:11>>] and bring forth the fruit of it in their lives.note:proof[<<Luke 8:15; Jas 1:25>>]

@ How do the sacraments become effectual means of salvation?

The sacraments become effectual means of salvation, not by any power in themselves, or any virtue derived from the piety or intention of him by whom they are administered, but only by the working of the Holy Ghost, and the blessing of Christ, by whom they are instituted.note:proof[<<1 Pet 3:21; Acts 8:13; Acts 8:23; 1 Cor 3:6–1 Cor 3:7; 1 Cor 12:13>>]

@ What is a sacrament?

A sacrament is a holy ordinance instituted by Christ in his church,note:proof[<<Gen 17:7; Gen 17:10; Exod 12; Matt 26:26–28; Matt 28:19>>] to signify, seal, and exhibit note:proof[<<Rom 4:11; 1 Cor 11:24–1 Cor 11:25>>] unto those that are within the covenant of grace,note:proof[<<Rom 15:8; Exod 12:48>>] the benefits of his mediation;note:proof[<<Acts 2:38; 1 Cor 10:16>>] to strengthen and increase their faith, and all other graces;note:proof[<<Rom 4:11; Gal 3:27>>] to oblige them to obedience;note:proof[<<Rom 6:3–4; 1 Cor 10:21>>] to testify and cherish their love and communion one with another;note:proof[<<Eph 4:2–5; 1 Cor 12:13>>] and to distinguish them from those that are without.note:proof[<<Eph 2:11–12; Gen 34:14>>]

@ What are the parts of a sacrament?

The parts of a sacrament are two; the one an outward and sensible sign, used according to Christ's own appointment; the other an inward and spiritual grace thereby signified.note:proof[<<Matt 3:11; 1 Pet 3:21; Rom 2:28–29>>]

@ How many sacraments hath Christ instituted in his church under the New Testament?

Under the New Testament Christ hath instituted in his church only two sacraments, Baptism and the Lord's supper.note:proof[<<Matt 26:26–28; Matt 28:19; 1 Cor 11:20; 1 Cor 11:23>>]

@ What is Baptism?

Baptism is a sacrament of the New Testament, wherein Christ hath ordained the washing with water in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost,note:proof[<<Matt 28:19>>] to be a sign and seal of ingrafting into himself,note:proof[<<Gal 3:27>>] of remission of sins by his blood,note:proof[<<Mark 1:4; Rev 1:5>>] and regeneration by his Spirit;note:proof[<<Titus 3:5; Eph 5:26>>] of adoption,note:proof[<<Gal 3:26–27>>] and resurrection unto everlasting life;note:proof[<<1 Cor 15:29; Rom 6:5>>] and whereby the parties baptized are solemnly admitted into the visible church,note:proof[<<1 Cor 12:13>>] and enter into an open and professed engagement to be wholly and only the Lord's.note:proof[<<Rom 6:4>>]

@ Unto whom is Baptism to be administered?

Baptism is not to be administered to any that are out of the visible church, and so strangers from the covenant of promise, till they profess their faith in Christ, and obedience to him,note:proof[<<Acts 2:38; Acts 8:36–37>>] but infants descending from parents, either both, or but one of them, professing faith in Christ, and obedience to him, are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized.note:proof[<<Gen 17:7; Gen 17:9; Gal 3:9; Gal 3:14; Col 2:11–12; Acts 2:38–39; Rom 4:11–12; Rom 11:16; 1 Cor 7:14; Matt 28:19; Luke 18:15–16>>]

@ How is our Baptism to be improved by us?

The needful but much neglected duty of improving our Baptism, is to be performed by us all our life long, especially in the time of temptation, and when we are present at the administration of it to others;note:proof[<<Col 2:11–12; Rom 6:4; Rom 6:6; Rom 6:11>>] by serious and thankful consideration of the nature of it, and of the ends for which Christ instituted it, the privileges and benefits conferred and sealed thereby, and our solemn vow made therein;note:proof[<<Rom 6:3–5>>] by being humbled for our sinful defilement, our falling short of, and walking contrary to, the grace of baptism, and our engagements;note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:11–1 Cor 1:13; Rom 6:2–3>>] by growing up to assurance of pardon of sin, and of all other blessings sealed to us in that sacrament;note:proof[<<Rom 4:11–12; 1 Pet 3:21>>] by drawing strength from the death and resurrection of Christ, into whom we are baptized, for the mortifying of sin, and quickening of grace;note:proof[<<Rom 6:3–5>>] and by endeavoring to live by faith,note:proof[<<Gal 3:26–27>>] to have our conversation in holiness and righteousness,note:proof[<<Rom 6:22>>] as those that have therein given up their names to Christ;note:proof[<<Acts 2:38>>] and to walk in brotherly love, as being baptized by the same Spirit into one body.note:proof[<<1 Cor 12:13; 1 Cor 12:25–1 Cor 12:27>>]

@ What is the Lord's supper?

The Lord's supper is a sacrament of the New Testament,note:proof[<<Luke 22:20>>] wherein, by giving and receiving bread and wine according to the appointment of Jesus Christ, his death is showed forth; and they that worthily communicate feed upon his body and blood, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace;note:proof[<<Matt 26:26–28; 1 Cor 11:13–1 Cor 11:26>>] have their union and communion with him confirmed;note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:16>>] testify and renew their thankfulness,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:24>>] and engagement to God,note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:14–1 Cor 10:16; 1 Cor 10:21>>] and their mutual love and fellowship each with other, as members of the same mystical body.note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:17>>]

@ How hath Christ appointed bread and wine to be given and received in the sacrament of the Lord's supper?

Christ hath appointed the ministers of his word, in the administration of this sacrament of the Lord's Supper, to set apart the bread and wine from common use, by the word of institution, thanksgiving, and prayer; to take and break the bread, and to give both the bread and the wine to the communicants: who are, by the same appointment, to take and eat the bread, and to drink the wine, in thankful remembrance that the body of Christ was broken and given, and his blood shed, for them.note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:23–1 Cor 11:24; Matt 26:26–28; Mark 14:22–24; Luke 22:19–20>>]

@ How do they that worthily communicate in the Lord's supper feed upon the body and blood of Christ therein?

As the body and blood of Christ are not corporally or carnally present in, with, or under the bread and wine in the Lord's supper,note:proof[<<Acts 3:21>>] and yet are spiritually present to the faith of the receiver, no less truly and really than the elements themselves are to their outward senses;note:proof[<<Matt 26:26; Matt 26:28>>] so they that worthily communicate in the sacrament of the Lord's supper, do therein feed upon the body and blood of Christ, not after a corporal and carnal, but in a spiritual manner; yet truly and really,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:24–1 Cor 11:29>>] while by faith they receive and apply unto themselves Christ crucified, and all the benefits of his death.note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:16>>]

@ How are they that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper to prepare themselves before they come unto it?

They that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper are, before they come, to prepare themselves thereunto, by examining themselves note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:28>>] of their being in Christ,note:proof[<<2 Cor 13:5>>] of their sins and wants;note:proof[<<1 Cor 5:7; Exod 12:15>>] of the truth and measure of their knowledge,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:29>>] faith,note:proof[<<1 Cor 13:5; Matt 26:28>>] repentance;note:proof[<<Zech 12:10; 1 Cor 11:31>>] love to God and the brethren,note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:16–1 Cor 10:17; Acts 2:46–47>>] charity to all men,note:proof[<<1 Cor 5:8; 1 Cor 11:18; 1 Cor 11:20>>] forgiving those that have done them wrong;note:proof[<<Matt 5:23–24>>] of their desires after Christ,note:proof[<<Isa 55:1; John 7:37>>] and of their new obedience;note:proof[<<1 Cor 5:7–1 Cor 5:8>>] and by renewing the exercise of these graces,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:25–1 Cor 11:26; 1 Cor 11:28; Heb 10:21–22; Heb 10:24; Ps 26:6>>] by serious meditation,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:24–1 Cor 11:25>>] and fervent prayer.note:proof[<<2 Chr 30:18–2 Chr 30:19; Matt 26:26>>]

@ May one who doubteth of his being in Christ, or of his due preparation, come to the Lord's supper?

One who doubteth of his being in Christ, or of his due preparation to the sacrament of the Lord's supper, may have true interest in Christ, though he be not yet assured thereof;note:proof[<<Isa 1:10; 1 John 5:13; Ps 88; Ps 77:1–12; Jonah 2:4; Jonah 2:7>>] and in God's account hath it, if he be duly affected with the apprehension of the want of it,note:proof[<<Isa 54:7–10; Matt 5:3–4; Ps 31:22; Ps 73:13; Ps 73:22–23>>] and unfeignedly desires to be found in Christ,note:proof[<<Phil 3:8–9; Ps 10:17; Ps 42:1–2; Ps 42:5; Ps 42:11>>] and to depart from iniquity:note:proof[<<2 Tim 2:19; Isa 1:10; Ps 66:18–20>>] in which case (because promises are made, and this sacrament is appointed, for the relief even of weak and doubting Christians)note:proof[<<Isa 40:11; Isa 40:29; Isa 40:31; Matt 11:28; Matt 12:20; Matt 26:28>>] he is to bewail his unbelief,note:proof[<<Mark 9:24>>] and labor to have his doubts resolved;note:proof[<<Acts 2:37; Acts 16:30>>] and, so doing, he may and ought to come to the Lord's supper, that he may be further strengthened.note:proof[<<Rom 4:11; 1 Cor 11:28>>]

@ May any who profess the faith, and desire to come to the Lord's supper, be kept from it?

Such as are found to be ignorant or scandalous, notwithstanding their profession of the faith, and desire to come to the Lord's supper, may and ought to be kept from that sacrament, by the power which Christ hath left in his church,note:proof[<<1 Cor 5; 1 Cor 11:27–1 Cor 11:31; Matt 7:6; Jude 1:23; 1 Tim 5:22>>] until they receive instruction, and manifest their reformation.note:proof[<<2 Cor 2:7>>]

@ What is required of them that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper in the time of the administration of it?

It is required of them that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper, that, during the time of the administration of it, with all holy reverence and attention they wait upon God in that ordinance,note:proof[<<Lev 10:3; Heb 12:18; Ps 5:7; 1 Cor 11:17; 1 Cor 11:26–1 Cor 11:27>>] diligently observe the sacramental elements and actions,note:proof[<<Exod 24:8; Matt 26:28>>] heedfully discern the Lord's body,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:29>>] and affectionately meditate on his death and sufferings,note:proof[<<Luke 22:19>>] and thereby stir up themselves to a vigorous exercise of their graces;note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:3–1 Cor 10:5; 1 Cor 10:11; 1 Cor 10:14; 1 Cor 11:26>>] in judging themselves,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:31>>] and sorrowing for sin;note:proof[<<Zech 12:10>>] in earnest hungering and thirsting after Christ,note:proof[<<Rev 22:17>>] feeding on him by faith,note:proof[<<John 6:35>>] receiving of his fulness,note:proof[<<John 1:16>>] trusting in his merits,note:proof[<<Phil 1:16>>] rejoicing in his love,note:proof[<<Ps 63:4–5; 2 Chr 30:21>>] giving thanks for his grace;note:proof[<<Ps 22:26>>] in renewing of their covenant with God, and love to all the saints.note:proof[<<Jer 1:5; Ps 1:5>>]

@ What is the duty of Christians, after they have received the sacrament of the Lord's supper?

The duty of Christians, after they have received the sacrament of the Lord's supper, is seriously to consider how they have behaved themselves therein, and with what success;note:proof[<<Ps 28:7; Ps 85:8; 1 Cor 11:17; 1 Cor 11:30–1 Cor 11:31>>] if they find quickening and comfort, to bless God for it,note:proof[<<2 Chr 30:21; 2 Chr 30:16; Acts 2:42; Acts 2:46>>] beg the continuance of it,note:proof[<<Ps 36:10; Song 3:4; 1 Chr 29:18>>] watch against relapses,note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:3–1 Cor 10:5; 1 Cor 10:12>>] fulfil their vows,note:proof[<<Ps 50:14>>] and encourage themselves to a frequent attendance on that ordinance:note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:25–1 Cor 11:26; Acts 2:42; Acts 2:46>>] but if they find no present benefit, more exactly to review their preparation to, and carriage at, the sacrament;note:proof[<<Song 5:1–6; Eccl 5:1–6>>] in both which, if they can approve themselves to God and their own consciences, they are to wait for the fruit of it in due time:note:proof[<<Ps 42:5; Ps 42:8; Ps 43:3–5; Ps 123:1–2>>] but, if they see they have failed in either, they are to be humbled,note:proof[<<2 Chr 30:18–2 Chr 30:19; Isa 1:16; Isa 1:18>>] and to attend upon it afterwards with more care and diligence.note:proof[<<2 Cor 7:11; 1 Chr 15:12–1 Chr 15:14>>]

@ Wherein do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper agree?

The sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper agree, in that the author of both is God;note:proof[<<Matt 28:19; 1 Cor 11:23>>] the spiritual part of both is Christ and his benefits;note:proof[<<Rom 6:3–4; 1 Cor 10:16>>] both are seals of the same covenant,note:proof[<<Rom 4:11; Col 2:12; Matt 26:27–28>>] are to be dispensed by ministers of the gospel, and by none other;note:proof[<<John 1:33; Matt 28:19; 1 Cor 4:1; 1 Cor 11:23; Heb 5:4>>] and to be continued in the church of Christ until his second coming.note:proof[<<Matt 28:19–20; 1 Cor 11:26>>]

@ Wherein do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper differ?

The sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper differ, in that baptism is to be administered but once, with water, to be a sign and seal of our regeneration and ingrafting into Christ,note:proof[<<Matt 3:11; Titus 3:5; Gal 3:27>>] and that even to infants;note:proof[<<Gen 17:7; Gen 17:9; Acts 2:38–39; 1 Cor 7:14>>] whereas the Lord's supper is to be administered often, in the elements of bread and wine, to represent and exhibit Christ as spiritual nourishment to the soul,note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:23–1 Cor 11:26>>] and to confirm our continuance and growth in him,note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:16>>] and that only to such as are of years and ability to examine themselves.note:proof[<<1 Cor 11:28–1 Cor 11:29>>]

@ What is prayer?

Prayer is an offering up of our desires unto God,note:proof[<<Ps 62:8>>] in the name of Christ,note:proof[<<John 16:23>>] by the help of his Spirit;note:proof[<<Rom 8:26>>] with confession of our sins,note:proof[<<Ps 32:5–6; Dan 9:4>>] and thankful acknowledgment of his mercies.note:proof[<<Phil 4:6>>]

@ Are we to pray unto God only?

God only being able to search the hearts,note:proof[<<1 Kgs 8:39; Acts 1:24; Rom 8:27>>] hear the requests,note:proof[<<Ps 65:2>>] pardon the sins,note:proof[<<Mic 7:18>>] and fulfil the desires of all;note:proof[<<Ps 145:18–19>>] and only to be believed in,note:proof[<<Rom 10:14>>] and worshiped with religious worship;note:proof[<<Matt 4:10>>] prayer, which is a special part thereof,note:proof[<<1 Cor 1:2>>] is to be made by all to him alone,note:proof[<<Ps 50:15>>] and to none other.note:proof[<<Rom 10:14>>]

@ What is it to pray in the name of Christ?

To pray in the name of Christ is, in obedience to his command, and in confidence on his promises, to ask mercy for his sake;note:proof[<<John 14:13–14; John 16:24; Dan 9:17>>] not by bare mentioning of his name,note:proof[<<Matt 7:21>>] but by drawing our encouragement to pray, and our boldness, strength, and hope of acceptance in prayer, from Christ and his mediation.note:proof[<<Heb 4:14–16; 1 John 5:13–1 John 5:15>>]

@ Why are we to pray in the name of Christ?

The sinfulness of man, and his distance from God by reason thereof, being so great, as that we can have no access into his presence without a mediator;note:proof[<<John 14:6; Isa 59:2; Eph 3:12>>] and there being none in heaven or earth appointed to, or fit for, that glorious work but Christ alone,note:proof[<<John 6:27; Heb 7:25–27; 1 Tim 2:5>>] we are to pray in no other name but his only.note:proof[<<Col 3:17; Heb 13:15>>]

@ How doth the Spirit help us to pray?

We not knowing: What to pray for as we ought, the Spirit helps our infirmities, by enabling us to understand both for whom, and: What, and: How prayer is to be made; and by working and quickening in our hearts (although not in all persons, nor at all times, in the same measure) those apprehensions, affections, and graces which are requisite for the right performance of that duty.note:proof[<<Rom 8:26–27; Ps 10:17; Zech 12:10>>]

@ For whom are we to pray?

We are to pray for the whole church of Christ upon earth;note:proof[<<Eph 6:18; Ps 28:9>>] for magistrates,note:proof[<<1 Tim 2:1–1 Tim 2:2>>] and ministers;note:proof[<<Col 4:3>>] for ourselves,note:proof[<<Gen 32:11>>] our brethren,note:proof[<<Jas 5:16>>] yea, our enemies;note:proof[<<Matt 5:44>>] and for all sorts of men living,note:proof[<<1 Tim 2:1–1 Tim 2:2>>] or that shall live hereafter;note:proof[<<John 17:20; 2 Sam 7:29>>] but not for the dead,note:proof[<<2 Sam 12:21–2 Sam 12:23>>] nor for those that are known to have sinned the sin unto death.note:proof[<<1 John 5:16>>]

@ For what things are we to pray?

We are to pray for all things tending to the glory of God,note:proof[<<Matt 6:9>>] the welfare of the church,note:proof[<<Ps 51:18; Ps 122:6>>] our own note:proof[<<Matt 7:11>>] or others good;note:proof[<<Ps 125:4>>] but not for anything that is unlawful.note:proof[<<1 John 5:14>>]

@ How are we to pray?

We are to pray with an awful apprehension of the majesty of God,note:proof[<<Eccl 5:1>>] and deep sense of our own unworthiness,note:proof[<<Gen 18:27; Gen 32:10>>] necessities,note:proof[<<Luke 15:17–19>>] and sins;note:proof[<<Luke 18:13–14>>] with penitent,note:proof[<<Ps 51:17>>] thankful,note:proof[<<Phil 4:6>>] and enlarged hearts;note:proof[<<1 Sam 1:15; 1 Sam 2:1>>] with understanding,note:proof[<<1 Cor 14:15>>] faith,note:proof[<<Mark 11:24; Jas 1:6>>] sincerity,note:proof[<<Ps 17:1; Ps 145:18>>] fervency,note:proof[<<Jas 5:16>>] love,note:proof[<<1 Tim 2:8>>] and perseverance,note:proof[<<Eph 6:18>>] waiting upon him,note:proof[<<Mic 7:7>>] with humble submission to his will.note:proof[<<Matt 26:39>>]

@ What rule hath God given for our direction in the duty of prayer?

The whole word of God is of use to direct us in the duty of prayer;note:proof[<<1 John 5:14>>] but the special rule of direction is that form of prayer which our Savior Christ taught his disciples, commonly called The Lord's prayer.note:proof[<<Matt 6:2–13; Luke 11:2–4>>]

@ How is the Lord's prayer to be used?

The Lord's prayer is not only for direction, as a pattern, according to which we are to make other prayers; but may also be used as a prayer, so that it be done with understanding, faith, reverence, and other graces necessary to the right performance of the duty of prayer.note:proof[<<Matt 6:9; Luke 11:2>>]

@ Of how many parts doth the Lord's prayer consist?

The Lord's prayer consists of three parts; a preface, petitions, and a conclusion.

@ What doth the preface of the Lord's prayer teach us?

The preface of the Lord's prayer (contained in these words, Our Father which art in heaven,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:9>>] teacheth us, when we pray, to draw near to God with confidence of his fatherly goodness, and our interest therein;note:proof[<<Luke 11:13; Rom 8:15>>] with reverence, and all other childlike dispositions,note:proof[<<Isa 64:9>>] heavenly affections,note:proof[<<Ps 123:1; Lam 3:41>>] and due apprehensions of his sovereign power, majesty, and gracious condescension:note:proof[<<Isa 63:15–16; Neh 1:4–6>>] as also, to pray with and for others.note:proof[<<Acts 12:5>>]

@ What do we pray for in the first petition?

In the first petition (which is, Hallowed be thy name,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:9>>] acknowledging the utter inability and indisposition that is in ourselves and all men to honor God aright,note:proof[<<2 Cor 3:5; Ps 51:15>>] pray, that God would by his grace enable and incline us and others to know, to acknowledge, and highly to esteem him,note:proof[<<Ps 67:2–3>>] his titles,note:proof[<<Ps 83:18>>] attributes,note:proof[<<Ps 86:10–13; Ps 86:15>>] ordinances, word,note:proof[<<2 Thess 3:1; Ps 138:1–3; Ps 147:19–20; 2 Cor 2:14–2 Cor 2:15>>] works, and whatsoever he is pleased to make himself known by;note:proof[<<Ps 145; Ps 8>>] and to glorify him in thought, word,note:proof[<<Ps 19:14; Ps 103:1>>] and deed:note:proof[<<Phil 1:9; Phil 1:11>>] that he would prevent and remove atheism,note:proof[<<Ps 67:1–4>>] ignorance,note:proof[<<Eph 1:17–18>>] idolatry,note:proof[<<Ps 97:7>>] profaneness,note:proof[<<Ps 74:18; Ps 74:22–23>>] and: whatsoever is dishonorable to him;note:proof[<<2 Kgs 19:15–2 Kgs 19:16>>] and, by his overruling providence, direct and dispose of all things to his own glory.note:proof[<<2 Chr 20:6; 2 Chr 20:10–2 Chr 20:12; Ps 83; Ps 140:4; Ps 140:8>>]

@ What do we pray for in the second petition.?

In the second petition (which is, Thy kingdom come,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:10>>] acknowledging ourselves and all mankind to be by nature under the dominion of sin and Satan,note:proof[<<Eph 2:2–3>>] we pray, that the kingdom of sin and Satan may be destroyed,note:proof[<<Ps 68:1; Ps 68:18; Rev 12:10–11>>] the gospel propagated throughout the world,note:proof[<<2 Thess 3:1>>] the Jews called,note:proof[<<Rom 10:1>>] the fulness of the Gentiles brought in;note:proof[<<John 17:9; John 17:20; Rom 11:25–26; Ps 67>>] the church furnished with all gospel officers and ordinances,note:proof[<<Matt 9:38; 2 Thess 3:1>>] purged from corruption,note:proof[<<Mal 1:11; Zeph 3:9>>] countenanced and maintained by the civil magistrate:note:proof[<<1 Tim 2:1–1 Tim 2:2>>] that the ordinances of Christ may be purely dispensed, and made effectual to the converting of those that are yet in their sins, and the confirming, comforting, and building up of those that are already converted:note:proof[<<Acts 4:29–30; Eph 6:18–20; Rom 15:29–30; Rom 15:32; 2 Thess 1:11; 2 Thess 2:16–2 Thess 2:17>>] that Christ would rule in our hearts here,note:proof[<<Eph 3:14–20>>] and hasten the time of his second coming, and our reigning with him forever:note:proof[<<Rev 22:20>>] and that he would be pleased so to exercise the kingdom of his power in all the world, as may best conduce to these ends.note:proof[<<Isa 64:1–2; Rev 4:8–11>>]

@ What do we pray for in the third petition?

In the third petition (which is, Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:10>>] acknowledging, that by nature we and all men are not only utterly unable and unwilling to know and do the will of God,note:proof[<<Rom 7:18; Job 21:14; 1 Cor 2:14>>] but prone to rebel against his word,note:proof[<<Rom 8:7>>] to repine and murmur against his providence,note:proof[<<Exod 17:7; Num 14:2>>] and wholly inclined to do the will of the flesh, and of the devil:note:proof[<<Eph 2:2>>] we pray, that God would by his Spirit take away from ourselves and others all blindness,note:proof[<<Eph 1:17–18>>] weakness,note:proof[<<Eph 3:16>>] indisposedness,note:proof[<<Matt 26:40–41>>] and perverseness of heart;note:proof[<<Jer 31:18–19>>] and by his grace make us able and willing to know, do, and submit to his will in all things,note:proof[<<Ps 119:1; Ps 119:8; Ps 119:35–36; Acts 21:14>>] with the like humility,note:proof[<<Mic 6:8>>] cheerfulness,note:proof[<<Ps 100:2; Job 1:21; 2 Sam 15:25–2 Sam 15:26>>] faithfulness,note:proof[<<Isa 38:3>>] diligence,note:proof[<<Ps 119:4–5>>] zeal,note:proof[<<Rom 12:11>>] sincerity,note:proof[<<Ps 119:80>>] and constancy,note:proof[<<Ps 119:112>>] as the angels do in heaven.note:proof[<<Isa 6:2–3; Ps 103:20–21; Matt 18:10>>]

@ What do we pray for in the fourth petition?

In the fourth petition (which is, Give us this day our daily bread,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:11>>] acknowledging, that in Adam, and by our own sin, we have forfeited our right to all the outward blessings of this life, and deserve to be wholly deprived of them by God, and to have them cursed to us in the use of them;note:proof[<<Gen 2:17; Gen 3:17; Rom 8:20–22; Jer 5:25; Deut 28:15–68>>] and that neither they of themselves are able to sustain us,note:proof[<<Deut 8:3>>] nor we to merit,note:proof[<<Gen 32:10>>] or by our own industry to procure them;note:proof[<<Deut 8:17–18>>] but prone to desire,note:proof[<<Jer 6:13; Mark 7:21–22>>] get,note:proof[<<Hos 12:7>>] and use them unlawfully:note:proof[<<Jas 4:3>>] we pray for ourselves and others, that both they and we, waiting upon the providence of God from day to day in the use of lawful means, may, of his free gift, and as to his fatherly wisdom shall seem best, enjoy a competent portion of them;note:proof[<<Gen 28:20; Gen 43:12–14; Eph 4:28; 2 Thess 3:11–2 Thess 3:12; Phil 4:6>>] and have the same continued and blessed unto us in our holy and comfortable use of them,note:proof[<<1 Tim 4:3–1 Tim 4:5>>] and contentment in them;note:proof[<<1 Tim 6:6–1 Tim 6:8>>] and be kept from all things that are contrary to our temporal support and comfort.note:proof[<<Prov 30:8–9>>]

@ What do we pray for in the fifth petition?

In the fifth petition (which is, Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:12>>] acknowledging, that we and all others are guilty both of original and actual sin, and thereby become debtors to the justice of God; and that neither we, nor any other creature, can make the least satisfaction for that debt:note:proof[<<Rom 3:9–22; Matt 18:24–25; Ps 130:3–4>>] we pray for ourselves and others, that God of his free grace would, through the obedience and satisfaction of Christ, apprehended and applied by faith, acquit us both from the guilt and punishment of sin,note:proof[<<Rom 3:24–26; Heb 9:22>>] accept us in his Beloved;note:proof[<<Eph 1:6–7>>] continue his favor and grace to us,note:proof[<<2 Pet 1:2>>] pardon our daily failings,note:proof[<<Hos 14:2; Jer 14:7>>] and fill us with peace and joy, in giving us daily more and more assurance of forgiveness;note:proof[<<Rom 15:13; Ps 51:7–10; Ps 51:12>>] which we are the rather emboldened to ask, and encouraged to expect, when we have this testimony in ourselves, that we from the heart forgive others their offenses.note:proof[<<Luke 11:4; Matt 6:14–15; Matt 18:35>>]

@ What do we pray for in the sixth petition?

In the sixth petition (which is, And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil,)note:proof[<<Matt 6:13>>] acknowledging, that the most wise, righteous, and gracious God, for divers holy and just ends, may so order things, that we may be assaulted, foiled, and for a time led captive by temptations;note:proof[<<2 Chr 32:31>>] that Satan,note:proof[<<1 Chr 21:1>>] the world,note:proof[<<Luke 21:34; Mark 4:19>>] and the flesh, are ready powerfully to draw us aside, and ensnare us;note:proof[<<Jas 1:14>>] and that we, even after the pardon of our sins, by reason of our corruption,note:proof[<<Gal 5:17>>] weakness, and want of watchfulness,note:proof[<<Matt 26:41>>] are not only subject to be tempted, and forward to expose ourselves unto temptations,note:proof[<<Matt 26:69–72; Gal 2:11–14; 2 Chr 18:3; 2 Chr 19:2>>] but also of ourselves unable and unwilling to resist them, to recover out of them, and to improve them;note:proof[<<Rom 7:23–24; 1 Chr 21:1–1 Chr 21:4; 2 Chr 16:7–2 Chr 16:10>>] and worthy to be left under the power of them:note:proof[<<Ps 81:11–12>>] we pray, that God would so overrule the world and all in it,note:proof[<<John 17:15>>] subdue the flesh,note:proof[<<Ps 51:10; Ps 119:133>>] and restrain Satan,note:proof[<<2 Cor 12:7–2 Cor 12:8>>] order all things,note:proof[<<1 Cor 10:12–1 Cor 10:13>>] bestow and bless all means of grace,note:proof[<<Heb 13:20–21>>] and quicken us to watchfulness in the use of them, that we and all his people may by his providence be kept from being tempted to sin;note:proof[<<Matt 26:41; Ps 19:13>>] or, if tempted, that by his Spirit we may be powerfully supported and enabled to stand in the hour of temptation;note:proof[<<Eph 3:14–17; 1 Thess 3:13; Jude 1:24>>] or when fallen, raised again and recovered out of it,note:proof[<<Ps 51:12>>] and have a sanctified use and improvement thereof:note:proof[<<1 Pet 5:8–1 Pet 5:10>>] that our sanctification and salvation may be perfected,note:proof[<<2 Cor 13:7; 2 Cor 13:9>>] Satan trodden under our feet,note:proof[<<Rom 16:20; Zech 3:2; Luke 22:31–32>>] and we fully freed from sin, temptation, and all evil, forever.note:proof[<<John 17:15; 1 Thess 5:23>>]

@ What doth the conclusion of the Lord's prayer teach us?

The conclusion of the Lord's prayer (which is, For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, forever. Amen.),note:proof[<<Matt 6:13>>] teaches us to enforce our petitions with arguments,note:proof[<<Rom 15:30>>] which are to be taken, not from any worthiness in ourselves, or in any other creature, but from God;note:proof[<<Dan 9:4; Dan 9:7–9; Dan 9:16–19>>] and with our prayers to join praises,note:proof[<<Phil 4:6>>] ascribing to God alone eternal sovereignty, omnipotency, and glorious excellency;note:proof[<<1 Chr 29:10–1 Chr 29:13>>] in regard whereof, as he is able and willing to help us,note:proof[<<Eph 3:20–21; Luke 11:13>>] so we by faith are emboldened to plead with him that he would,note:proof[<<2 Chr 20:6; 2 Chr 20:11>>] and quietly to rely upon him, that he will fulfil our requests.note:proof[<<2 Chr 14:11>>] And, to testify this our desire and assurance, we say, Amen.note:proof[<<1 Cor 14:16; Rev 22:20–21>>]
